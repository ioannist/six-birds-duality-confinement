\section{Lean formalization}
\label{app:formalization}

This appendix records how the Lean development is cited by the RH
paper.  The governing rule is that the prose must distinguish a
theorem proved in Lean, a definition realized as a Lean structure, a
recognition source encoded as a typed carrier, and material merely
tracked by the formalization harness.  The body sections use
paper-level mathematical names except where a disclosure explicitly
requires Lean syntax; full Lean identifiers and module-level
bookkeeping appear here.

\subsection*{Typed positive-cone encoding}

The formalization uses the same mathlib-free typed positive-cone
interface as \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}.  The interface records exactly
the primitives used by the arguments: an order \(\preceq\), a trace
functional \(\trace\), trace monotonicity under the order,
nonnegativity of the relevant scalar summands, and the
positivity-to-zero principles that turn vanishing trace or vanishing
square into vanishing obstruction.  No full Hilbert-space operator
theory is imported; the point of the encoding is to isolate the
trace-order content needed by the duality-confinement membrane
theorem and by the RH translation argument.

In the present paper this typed cone is the substrate on which the
anti-invariant zero ledger \(\AZ\) lives.  Theorem~T is a finite
typed-multiset argument over the cone: \(\AZ\) is the
multiplicity-weighted sum of squared anti-invariant readout values,
so \(\AZ=0\) is equivalent to every typed zero lying on the critical
line.  The bridge theorem \texttt{dcMasterApplied} then applies the
master theorem of \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026} to this RH-specific typed
cone.  A full operator-theoretic realization over trace-class
operators or over the classical Selberg trace formula apparatus is
an extension target, not a hidden claim of the Lean development.

\subsection*{Representation note A3: real-part coordinate}

The RH formalization contains one representation note specific to the
zero ledger.  The paper definition of \(\AZ\) uses the classical
expression \(\Real(\rho)-1/2\).  In the mathlib-free Lean
development, however, the real-part coordinate is parameterized by a
typed carrier \texttt{RealCoordinate}, not by the standard
\(\mathbb{R}\) with analytic-number-theoretic infrastructure.  The
carrier supplies the algebraic structure needed for the proof:
\(1/2\), subtraction from \(1/2\), squaring, nonnegativity of squares,
and the implication that a zero square displacement forces equality
with \(1/2\).

The identification of \texttt{RealCoordinate} with the actual
\(\mathbb{R}\)-valued real part of a classical nontrivial zero of
\(\zeta\) is made by construction-parameter assignment when the
shell \(\Sel\) is instantiated.  It is not a derived Lean predicate.
This is forced by the mathlib-free constraint: the development does
not import the standard real-analysis and complex-analytic
infrastructure needed to prove inside Lean that the typed coordinate
equals the classical real-part function on zeta zeros.  The body
discloses this point in the ledger section; the present appendix
records the encoding rationale.  A substantive Lean derivation of
this identification is one of the open extensions named in
\Cref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection*{Recognition-source carrier (typed bridge)}

The recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is the distinctive
formal object of the RH paper.  It is realized in Lean as a typed
structure carrier \texttt{GammaSdtcSelberg} that packages, in one
record, the entire duality-confinement apparatus needed by the
bridge theorem (involutive object ledger, separating
anti-invariant readout, anti-invariant ledger, completed-domination
records, and the typed-cone primitives \(\preceq\), \(\trace\),
positivity-to-zero) together with the bridge propositions
\texttt{same\_readout}, \texttt{mu\_zero\_of\_ae}, and
\texttt{visible\_zero\_of\_ae}.  These bridge propositions express
the assertion that the supplied DC apparatus is coherent with the
shell---in particular, that \(\psim\) vanishing \(\mu\)-almost
everywhere implies \(\AZ = 0\) on the shell.  They are typed
hypotheses, not derivations: the carrier supplies the DC apparatus
together with its claimed coherence with the shell, not a
construction of the DC apparatus from the shell's own data.  The
domination-record content witnesses
\[
  \AZ \preceq B_n,
  \qquad
  \trace B_n \to 0 .
\]

The carrier is declared inline in the Lean module that consumes
it, \texttt{RHConditional.lean}, following the placement
convention that a type with a single consumer lives next to that
consumer.  It is not a Lean axiom.  The project-wide
forbidden-tokens rule bans \texttt{axiom}, \texttt{opaque},
\texttt{constant}, \texttt{sorry}, and \texttt{admit} throughout
the source tree.  The recognition source is therefore supplied as
a typed-record hypothesis, not as a postulated logical axiom, and
the conditional landing-chain theorem \texttt{rhConditional} takes
a value of this carrier as an explicit hypothesis parameter,
\(\gamma : \texttt{GammaSdtcSelberg shell}\).  This is the formal
counterpart of the structural-law discipline in the body: closure
content of a formed layer is named as a recognition source, not
added as a metalogical postulate.

\subsection*{Cross-paper Lean import}

The present paper imports the duality-confinement master theorem
from \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}.  The Lean theorem
\texttt{dcMasterApplied} imports \texttt{masterTheorem} from the
module \nolinkurl{DualityConfinement.MasterTheorem} of that source
and composes it through a typed-bridge interface: the DC apparatus
(involutive object ledger, separating readout, anti-invariant
ledger, completed-domination records, typed-cone primitives) is
supplied as input rather than constructed from \(\Sel\), and three
bridge fields link the apparatus to the shell---a same-readout
coherence, a visible-support refinement, and the implication
\(\mathtt{mu\_zero\_of\_ae}\) translating ``\(\psim\) vanishes
\(\mu\)-almost everywhere'' into the shell equality \(\AZ=0\).  The
body-level role assignment (\(\Sel\leftrightarrow\IOL\),
\(\AZ\leftrightarrow\AX\), \(\JL\leftrightarrow J\),
\(\psimRH\leftrightarrow\psim\), zero measure \(\leftrightarrow\mu\))
is the mathematical interpretation of this bridge interface, not a
Lean derivation of the role assignment.  This is a one-way Lean
import, matching the paper-level dependency on the SDTC apparatus of
\cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}.

\subsection*{AOR-instance coverage}

The AOR-instance recasting of \Cref{sec:rh-aor-instance} adds nine
entries to the formalization: one definition (the per-shell
AOR-instance carrier \texttt{SelAORInstance} together with the
canonical assembled value \texttt{assembledCarrier}), seven
literal-data discharge definitions covering the atoms of the
twelve-type residual classification that the Selberg shell
consumes, and one composing theorem (the main AOR-instance
assertion \texttt{aorInstance}).  All nine entries live in the Lean
module \nolinkurl{RH.AORInstance}.

Two disclosure points apply uniformly to the seven discharge rows.
First, each discharge is realized in Lean as a \texttt{def}
returning a literal classification record indexed by the shell and
the recognition source but not computed from PDE-style data; this
matches the body presentation of \Cref{def:rh:aor-sel-instance}, in
which the eight-stratum / twelve-type residual atlas is read off
carrier fields rather than derived.  Second, the substantive
recognition-source and translation-interface discharges are
deferred to \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026} and
\Cref{sec:translation_theorem} respectively, so the seven discharge
entries are typed-interface records of the AOR-instance object,
not independent derivations.

The main AOR-instance theorem
\nolinkurl{RH.AORInstance.aorInstance} composes the seven literal
discharges through the local refinement-stable AOR predicate
\nolinkurl{RH.AORPrimitives.RefStableAOR}; its conclusion is the
\texttt{Prop}-valued statement
\(\mathrm{RefStableAOR}(\texttt{assembledCarrier}\,\Sel\,\gamma)\),
which is the local syntactic predicate over the carrier fields and
\emph{not} the full audit-closure refinement-stable
characterisation of \citet{TsiokosAOR2026}.  The
\texttt{nonclaims\_nonempty} structural witness is supplied by the
discharge definitions; the proof obligation per atom dispatches by
primary residual type through the seven discharge defs and routes
each forced-secondary entry through the literal classification.  No
substantive PDE-style audit content is added.

\subsection*{No-smuggling gates}

The final audit layer is paper-side discipline that checks the
conditional theorem says only what its hypotheses support, with no
structural input hidden in the prose.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,labelsep=0.75em,itemsep=0.35em]
\item[Gate 1: no overreading.]
The conclusion is conditional on \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) and on
Foundations-II admissibility of \(\Sel\).  The typed-ledger
conclusion is exactly the parameterized statement
\(\rho\in\Znt \Rightarrow \Real(\rho)=1/2\).

\item[Gate 2: no undisclosed hypothesis.]
Every load-bearing assumption is named: the recognition source
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\), the \(\mathrm{Audit}_L\) admissibility field,
the typed real-coordinate identification, and the typed positive-cone
primitives.

\item[Gate 3: no hidden mechanism.]
The proof composition is explicit: the recognition source supplies
domination records, \Cref{thm:rh:dc-master-applied} yields
\(\AZ=0\), and \Cref{thm:rh:translation-T-forward} yields the
critical-line statement on \(\Znt\).

\item[Gate 4: no conflation of grades.]
Recognition-source content and theorem-grade content are separated.
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is a recognition source; Theorem~T is a direct
positivity theorem; \texttt{dcMasterApplied} is the theorem-level
application of the master theorem of \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}; and
\texttt{rhConditional} is the theorem-grade conditional
composition.

\item[Gate 5: no axiom smuggling.]
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is a typed structure carrier, not a Lean axiom.
The forbidden-tokens audit excludes \texttt{axiom}, \texttt{opaque},
\texttt{constant}, \texttt{sorry}, and \texttt{admit} from active
Lean source.

\item[Gate 6: no undisclosed cross-paper import.]
The use of \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026} is explicit: the body reproduces
the duality-confinement master theorem statement, and
\texttt{dcMasterApplied} is the named Lean-side cross-paper import.

\item[Gate 7: recognition-source provenance.]
The recognition source is documented as the Selberg-class instance
of the SDTC structural law of \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}, and the
three-route derivation sweep summarized in
\Cref{sec:recognition_source} confirms that framework primitives do
not derive it.  This is the recognition-source provenance audit; it
is the no-overreading suppression check in the Foundations-III
audited vocabulary \cite{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026}.
\end{description}

These seven checks are part of the paper's audit presentation.  They
do not add hypotheses to the Lean theorems, and they are not
themselves advertised as Lean-mechanized predicates.
