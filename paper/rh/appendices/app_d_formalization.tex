\section{Lean formalization}
\label{app:formalization}

This appendix records how the Lean development is cited by the RH
paper.  The governing rule is that the prose must distinguish a
theorem proved in Lean, a definition realized as a Lean structure, a
recognition source encoded as a typed carrier, and material merely
tracked by the formalization harness.  The body sections use
paper-level mathematical names except where a disclosure explicitly
requires Lean syntax; full Lean identifiers and module-level
bookkeeping appear here.

\subsection*{Typed positive-cone encoding}

The formalization uses the same mathlib-free typed positive-cone
interface as \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}: an order \(\preceq\), a trace
functional \(\trace\), trace monotonicity, nonnegativity of the scalar
summands, and positivity-to-zero principles.  This is the substrate on
which \(\AZ\) lives.  Theorem~T is a finite typed-multiset argument over
the cone, and \texttt{dcMasterApplied} applies the master theorem of
\cite{TsiokosSDTC2026} to the RH-specific cone.  Full operator-theoretic
realizations over trace-class operators \citep{Simon2005} or the
classical Selberg trace formula apparatus \citep{Hejhal1976,Hejhal1983}
remain extension targets.

\subsection*{Representation note A3: real-part coordinate}

The paper definition of \(\AZ\) uses \(\Real(\rho)-1/2\).  In the
mathlib-free Lean development, the real-part coordinate is parameterized
by \texttt{RealCoordinate}, not by the standard \(\mathbb{R}\) with
analytic-number-theoretic infrastructure.  The carrier supplies the
algebraic structure needed here: \(1/2\), subtraction from \(1/2\),
squaring, nonnegativity of squares, and the implication that a zero
square displacement forces equality with \(1/2\).

The identification of \texttt{RealCoordinate} with the actual
\(\mathbb{R}\)-valued real part of a classical nontrivial zero of
\(\zeta\) is fixed by construction-parameter assignment when \(\Sel\)
is instantiated.  It is not a derived Lean predicate; deriving it would
require real- and complex-analytic infrastructure outside the
mathlib-free development.  This is the A3 representation note referenced
from \Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger,sec:discussion}.

\subsection*{Representation note: admissibility as \(\mathrm{Audit}_L\)}

The paper definition of the SAT-SEL shell \(\Sel\) requires
admissibility under the seven Foundations-II audit schemas.  In the
Lean development this admissibility is not a separately-derived
predicate but is carried by the \(\mathrm{Audit}_L\) field of the
\nolinkurl{SatSelShell} structure: a value of \nolinkurl{SatSelShell}
packages the shell data together with a typed proof object asserting
audit-closure under the seven schemas.  Downstream theorems consume
this field as a hypothesis rather than rederiving it.  This is the
admissibility representation note referenced from
\Cref{sec:sat_sel_shell}.

\subsection*{Recognition-source carrier (typed bridge)}

The recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is realized in Lean as the
typed structure carrier \nolinkurl{GammaSdtcSelberg}.  The record packages
the duality-confinement apparatus needed by the bridge theorem
(involutive object ledger, separating anti-invariant readout,
anti-invariant ledger, completed-domination records, and typed-cone
primitives) together with the bridge propositions
\nolinkurl{same_readout}, \nolinkurl{mu_zero_of_ae}, and
\nolinkurl{visible_zero_of_ae}.  These propositions assert coherence
with the shell, including the implication from \(\psim\) vanishing
\(\mu\)-almost everywhere to \(\AZ=0\).  They are typed hypotheses, not
derivations from the shell's own data.  The domination-record content
witnesses
\[
  \AZ \preceq B_n,
  \qquad
  \trace B_n \to 0 .
\]

The carrier is declared inline in \texttt{RHConditional.lean}, its only
consumer.  It is not a Lean axiom: the project-wide forbidden-tokens
rule bans \texttt{axiom}, \texttt{opaque}, \texttt{constant},
\texttt{sorry}, and \texttt{admit}.  The theorem
\texttt{rhConditional} therefore takes
\(\gamma : \texttt{GammaSdtcSelberg shell}\) as an explicit hypothesis
parameter.  This mirrors the body discipline: formed-layer closure
content is named as a recognition source, not added as a metalogical
postulate.

\subsection*{Cross-paper Lean import}

The Lean theorem \texttt{dcMasterApplied} imports
\texttt{masterTheorem} from
\nolinkurl{DualityConfinement.MasterTheorem} in
\cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}.  The DC apparatus is supplied as input rather
than constructed from \(\Sel\), and the bridge fields express
same-readout coherence, visible-support refinement, and
\(\mathtt{mu\_zero\_of\_ae}\), which translates ``\(\psim\) vanishes
\(\mu\)-almost everywhere'' into \(\AZ=0\).  The body-level role
assignment (\(\Sel\leftrightarrow\IOL\), \(\AZ\leftrightarrow\AX\),
\(\JL\leftrightarrow J\), \(\psimRH\leftrightarrow\psim\), zero measure
\(\leftrightarrow\mu\)) is the mathematical interpretation of this
bridge interface.  This is a one-way Lean import.

\subsection*{AOR-instance coverage}

The AOR-instance recasting of \Cref{sec:rh-aor-instance} adds nine
entries: \texttt{SelAORInstance} and the assembled carrier, seven
literal-data discharge definitions, and the composing theorem
\texttt{aorInstance}.  All live in \nolinkurl{RH.AORInstance}.  Each
discharge definition returns one or more \texttt{DischargeAtom} values
read from the Selberg-shell carrier fields; atoms outside the
Selberg-shell consumption set do not appear in the assembled list.
The substantive recognition-source and translation-interface content is
deferred to \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026} and
\Cref{sec:translation_theorem}, respectively.

The theorem \nolinkurl{RH.AORInstance.aorInstance} composes the seven
literal discharges through the local \texttt{RefStableAOR} predicate on
the assembled carrier.  This is a local syntactic predicate over carrier
fields, not the full audit-closure refinement-stable characterisation of
\citet{TsiokosAOR2026}.  The proof dispatches by primary residual type
and routes forced-secondary entries through the literal classifications.

\subsection*{No-smuggling gates}

The final audit layer is paper-side discipline that checks the
conditional theorem says only what its hypotheses support, with no
structural input hidden in the prose.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,labelsep=0.75em,itemsep=0.35em]
\item[Gate 1: no overreading.]
The conclusion is conditional on \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) and on
Foundations-II admissibility of \(\Sel\).  The typed-ledger
conclusion is exactly the parameterized statement
\(\rho\in\Znt \Rightarrow \Real(\rho)=1/2\).

\item[Gate 2: no undisclosed hypothesis.]
Every load-bearing assumption is named: the recognition source
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\), the \(\mathrm{Audit}_L\) admissibility field,
the typed real-coordinate identification, and the typed positive-cone
primitives.

\item[Gate 3: no hidden mechanism.]
The proof composition is explicit: the recognition source supplies
domination records, \Cref{thm:rh:dc-master-applied} yields
\(\AZ=0\), and \Cref{thm:rh:translation-T-forward} yields the
critical-line statement on \(\Znt\).

\item[Gate 4: no conflation of grades.]
Recognition-source content and theorem-grade content are separated.
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is a recognition source; Theorem~T is a direct
positivity theorem; \texttt{dcMasterApplied} is the theorem-level
application of the master theorem of \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}; and
\texttt{rhConditional} is the theorem-grade conditional
composition.

\item[Gate 5: no axiom smuggling.]
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is a typed structure carrier, not a Lean axiom.
The forbidden-tokens audit excludes \texttt{axiom}, \texttt{opaque},
\texttt{constant}, \texttt{sorry}, and \texttt{admit} from active
Lean source.

\item[Gate 6: no undisclosed cross-paper import.]
The use of \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026} is explicit: the body reproduces
the duality-confinement master theorem statement, and
\texttt{dcMasterApplied} is the named Lean-side cross-paper import.

\item[Gate 7: recognition-source provenance.]
The recognition source is documented as the Selberg-class instance
of the SDTC structural law of \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}, and the
three-route derivation sweep summarized in
\Cref{sec:recognition_source} confirms that framework primitives do
not derive it.  This is the recognition-source provenance audit; it
is the no-overreading suppression check in the Foundations-III
audited vocabulary \cite{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026}.
\end{description}

These seven checks are part of the paper's audit presentation.  They
do not add hypotheses to the Lean theorems, and they are not
themselves advertised as Lean-mechanized predicates.
