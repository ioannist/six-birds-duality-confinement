\begin{abstract}
This paper gives a conditional Six Birds closure of the Riemann
hypothesis by moving the question from classical analytic number
theory to a formed-layer trace setting.  The substrate is the
saturated completed Selberg trace closure of the completed Riemann
zeta function, equipped with the functional-equation involution and
with a typed ledger of nontrivial zeros.  A translation theorem T
shows that the anti-invariant zero ledger of the completed zeta
shell vanishes, \(\AZ=0\), if and only if every typed nontrivial
zero has real part \(1/2\).  The conditional
landing chain uses the Selberg-class instance of Self-Dual Trace
Confinement supplied by \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}: its recognition
source gives domination records whose vanishing trace forces
\(\AZ=0\), and theorem T converts that ledger collapse into the
critical-line statement.
Stated in the audit-closure language of \citet{TsiokosAOR2026},
the same conditional chain is recorded as a local
refinement-stable audited-operational-realisability instance,
\(\Sel\in\mathrm{RefStableAOR}^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathfrak S_{\mathrm{RH}}}\),
whose typed-zero-ledger readout is the critical-line statement.

Outside Six Birds, the result is the conditional theorem
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{SDTC}\text{-}\mathrm{Selberg}}\Rightarrow
\mathrm{RH}\) on the typed zero ledger.  This is not an unconditional
proof of the Riemann hypothesis in standard ZFC.
The shell's admissibility is a standing Foundations-II hypothesis, and
the real-part coordinate of a typed zero is a representation parameter
identified with the classical real part by construction-parameter
assignment.  The development is formalized in Lean as a mathlib-free
mechanization (Appendix~\ref{app:formalization}).
\end{abstract}
