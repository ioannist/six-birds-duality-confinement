\begin{abstract}
This paper gives a conditional Six Birds closure of the Riemann
hypothesis by moving the question from classical analytic number
theory to a formed-layer trace setting.  The substrate is the
saturated completed Selberg trace closure of the completed Riemann
zeta function, equipped with the functional-equation involution and
with a typed ledger of nontrivial zeros.  The auxiliary translation
theorem T proves that the anti-invariant zero ledger vanishes,
\(A_Z(\zeta)=0\), if and only if every typed nontrivial zero has real
part \(1/2\).  The conditional landing chain then uses the
Selberg-class instance of Self-Dual Trace Confinement supplied by
\citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}: its recognition source gives domination
records whose vanishing trace forces \(A_Z(\zeta)=0\), and theorem
T converts that ledger collapse into the critical-line statement.

The outside-Six-Birds reading is the conditional theorem
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{SDTC}\text{-}\mathrm{Selberg}}\Rightarrow
\mathrm{RH}\), not an unconditional proof of the Riemann hypothesis
in standard ZFC.  The saturated trace shell's admissibility is a
standing Foundations-II hypothesis, and the formal zero ledger uses
a typed real-coordinate carrier whose identification with the
classical real part of a zeta zero is fixed by construction-parameter
assignment.  The recognition source is encoded in Lean as a typed
structure carrier, not as a Lean axiom.  The mathlib-free
formalization includes ten theorems and definitions---the
translation theorem, the duality-confinement bridge, the conditional
landing chain, and supporting structure---together with the
recognition-source carrier, each faithful to its paper-side
statement under the disclosures recorded in the formalization
appendix.
\end{abstract}
