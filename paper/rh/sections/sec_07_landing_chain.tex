\section{The conditional landing chain}
\label{sec:landing_chain}

The previous section identified the structural input needed by the RH
closure: the recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) supplies a
sequence of domination records for the anti-invariant zero ledger
\(\AZ\).  The present section begins the landing chain by importing
the one abstract theorem from the sibling paper that acts on such a
sequence.  Paper 1 \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026} states the following
duality-confinement membrane theorem, reproduced verbatim here for
reference.

\begin{theorem}[Duality-confinement membrane theorem]
Let \(\IOL\) be an involutive object ledger with carrier \(X\),
involution \(J\), measure \(\mu\), anti-invariant readout
\(\psim\), and anti-invariant ledger \(\AX\).  Assume that
\(\psim\) is qualitatively separating.  Suppose there is a sequence
of positive cone elements \(B_n\) such that
\[
  \AX\preceq B_n
  \qquad (n=1,2,\ldots)
\]
and
\[
  \trace B_n\to0
  \qquad (n\to\infty).
\]
Then \(\AX=0\) in the typed positive cone.  Consequently,
\[
  \mu\bigl(X\setminus\Fix(J)\bigr)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

Two points about the imported theorem are important for the RH
application.  First, it is a typed positive-cone statement: it needs
an order \(\preceq\), a trace functional \(\trace\), and a separating
anti-invariant readout, but it does not ask for an independent
classical analytic proof of RH.  Second, the theorem consumes
domination records; it does not supply their source.  In the present
paper that source is exactly \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), introduced in
\Cref{sec:recognition_source}.

We now apply this theorem to the Selberg-class instance.  The
abstract involutive object ledger becomes the saturated completed
Selberg trace closure \(\Sel\) restricted to its typed zero ledger.
The abstract carrier \(X\) is the zero-carrier underlying \(\Znt\);
the involution \(J\) becomes the functional-equation involution
\(\JL\); the measure \(\mu\) becomes the multiplicity-weighted zero
measure; the separating readout \(\psim\) becomes the signed
real-part displacement \(\psimRH\); and the anti-invariant ledger
\(\AX\) becomes the anti-invariant zero ledger \(\AZ\).

\begin{theorem}[Duality-confinement master theorem, applied to \(\Sel\)]
\label{thm:rh:dc-master-applied}
Let \(\Sel\) be the saturated completed Selberg trace closure with
functional-equation involution \(\JL\), separating anti-invariant
readout \(\psimRH\), and anti-invariant zero ledger \(\AZ\).  Suppose
there is a sequence \(B_n\) of positive cone elements such that
\[
  \AZ\preceq B_n
  \qquad (n=1,2,\ldots)
\]
and
\[
  \trace B_n\to0
  \qquad (n\to\infty).
\]
Then \(\AZ=0\) in the typed positive cone associated to \(\Sel\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
This is the duality-confinement membrane theorem of Paper 1
\cite{TsiokosSDTC2026} applied under the substitutions just listed.
The qualitative-separation hypothesis is the concrete calculation of
\Cref{def:rh:psi-minus-rh}: \(\psimRH(s)=0\) exactly on
\(\Fix(\JL)\), and \(\psimRH(\JL(s))=-\psimRH(s)\).  The domination
records are precisely the records supplied by the recognition source
of \Cref{obl:rh:gamma-sdtc-selberg}.  Once the records give
\(\AZ\preceq B_n\) and \(\trace B_n\to0\), the typed-cone squeeze of
Paper 1 forces \(\AZ=0\).
\end{proof}

The bridge theorem is therefore the point at which the conditional
input from \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) becomes the concrete ledger equality
\(\AZ=0\).  In the notation of Paper 1, the fixed-locus consequence
would say that the typed zero-ledger mass is confined to
\(\Fix(\JL)\), the critical line.  In the notation of the present
paper, Theorem T gives the sharper pointwise formulation on the
finite typed ledger: every \(\rho\in\Znt\) has
\(\Real(\rho)=1/2\).

Lean derives this RH-specific application of the master theorem, as
recorded in the formalization appendix.  The formal proof imports the
Paper 1 master theorem and instantiates it on the typed zero ledger
through the bridge parameter carrying the shell-specific measure and
readout data into the master theorem's hypotheses.

The pointwise critical-line conclusion is now one step away: the
headline conditional theorem composes \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), the bridge
theorem above, and the forward direction of Theorem T.

Composing the recognition source from \Cref{sec:recognition_source}
with the bridge theorem above and the forward direction of Theorem T
yields the headline conditional landing chain.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional landing chain]
\label{thm:rh:conditional}
Let \(\Sel\) be a saturated completed Selberg trace closure.  Under
the standing assumption that \(\Sel\) is Foundations-II admissible,
and given the recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), the
Selberg-class instance of Self-Dual Trace Confinement supplied by
Paper 1 \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}, the Riemann hypothesis holds on the
typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) of \(\Sel\): every typed nontrivial zero
\(\rho\in\Znt\) satisfies
\[
  \Real(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof is a direct three-step chain.  First, the recognition
source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), as stated in
\Cref{obl:rh:gamma-sdtc-selberg}, supplies a sequence \(B_n\) of
positive cone elements with
\[
  \AZ\preceq B_n,
  \qquad
  \trace B_n\to0.
\]
Second, \Cref{thm:rh:dc-master-applied} applies the
duality-confinement master theorem to this Selberg-class instance and
therefore gives \(\AZ=0\) in the typed positive cone associated to
\(\Sel\).  Third, the forward direction of translation theorem T,
\Cref{thm:rh:translation-T-forward}, converts \(\AZ=0\) into
\[
  \forall\,\rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}.
\]
\end{proof}

The conditional structure of this result is essential.  The theorem
asserts that, under the standing Foundations-II admissibility of
\(\Sel\) recorded in \Cref{def:rh:sat-sel-shell}, under the Six Birds
closure-content discipline that treats \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) as
supplied structural content of the formed self-dual Selberg trace
layer, and under the typed real-coordinate identification described in
\Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger}, every typed nontrivial zero of the
saturated Selberg trace shell lies on the critical line.  Outside the
Six Birds framing, the result reads as the conditional theorem
\[
  \GamSDTCSelberg \Longrightarrow \mathrm{RH},
\]
not as an unconditional proof of RH in standard ZFC.  The conditional
structure is preserved by the formalization: the conditional landing
chain takes a value of the recognition-source carrier as an explicit
hypothesis parameter.

The same conclusion can also be rendered in the framework's BirdInt
judgment form.  A BirdInt judgment is a framework-relative typed
sequent: it packages a source, a formed layer, an instrument, and a
proposition together with the audit data that records the judgment's
status \cite{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026}.  In the present case the
judgment reads schematically as
\[
  \GamSDTCSelberg;\ \Sel;\ \Itr
  \;\vdash\;
  \bigl(\forall\,\rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)
  : \mathrm{accepted}.
\]
This framework rendering is not load-bearing for the proof above; it
records the same conditional theorem in the audited judgment
vocabulary used by Foundations III.

Lean proves the conditional landing chain, as recorded in the
formalization appendix, with the recognition source supplied as an
explicit hypothesis parameter.  The formal realization takes a value
of the typed-structure carrier representing \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) as
input, applies the bridge theorem
\Cref{thm:rh:dc-master-applied}, and then applies the forward
direction of translation theorem T,
\Cref{thm:rh:translation-T-forward}.  The resulting conclusion is the
typed-ledger statement that every typed nontrivial zero of \(\Sel\)
lies on the critical line.

This completes the conditional landing chain.  The scope, nonclaim
discipline, structural-program positioning, and pending formalization
extensions are the subject of \Cref{sec:scope_and_nonclaims} and the
sections that follow.
