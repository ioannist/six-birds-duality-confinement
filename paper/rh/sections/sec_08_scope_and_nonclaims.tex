\section{Scope and non-claims}
\label{sec:scope_and_nonclaims}

Several scope boundaries deserve explicit statement so that the
conditional landing chain of \Cref{sec:landing_chain} remains sharp.
The point is not to weaken the theorem just proved, but to identify
which structural content is assumed, which encoding choices are
made, and which extensions remain outside the present paper.

\paragraph{NC-1. Conditional structure.}
The headline result is a Six Birds-native conditional theorem under
the standard Six Birds closure assumption.  Outside the Six Birds
framing, it reads as
\[
  \GamSDTCSelberg \Longrightarrow \mathrm{RH}.
\]
That is, given the Selberg-class recognition source, the conditional
landing chain gives RH on the typed zero ledger of \(\Sel\).  This
paper does not claim a standard-ZFC unconditional proof of the
Riemann hypothesis.  Every later use of ``RH'' in this paper should
therefore be read either as the typed-ledger statement under the
construction-parameter identification, or as the conclusion of this
conditional implication.

\paragraph{NC-2. Recognition-source provenance.}
The recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is the Selberg-class
instance of the Self-Dual Trace Confinement structural law of
\citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}.  It is not derived from framework
primitives; the three-route sweep over candidate primitive paths
discussed in \Cref{sec:recognition_source} documents that none of
them produces the trace-vanishing domination sequence.

\paragraph{NC-3. Parameter identification.}
The typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) is a typed multiset parameter of the
shell.  The formal encoding parameterizes the real-part coordinate by
a typed real-coordinate carrier, and the identification of that
coordinate with the classical \(\Real(\rho)\) for a nontrivial zero
\(\rho\) of \(\zeta\) is made by construction-parameter assignment,
as disclosed in \Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger}.  Under that
assignment, the conclusion \(\Real(\rho)=1/2\) on the typed ledger is
the usual RH statement.  A formal derivation of this identification
from analytic-number-theoretic infrastructure is an open extension.
This is a math representation choice, not a change to the
mathematical content being represented.

\paragraph{NC-4. Admissibility as hypothesis.}
Admissibility of the saturated completed Selberg trace closure
\(\Sel\) with respect to the seven Foundations-II admissibility
schemas is carried by the audit field of \(\Sel\), as described in
\Cref{def:rh:sat-sel-shell} \cite{TsiokosFoundationsII2026}.  It is
a construction-time hypothesis of the typed shell, not a theorem
derived here.  A substantive formal derivation would require
unfolding the seven schemas as predicates and verifying the
completed Selberg trace instance field by field.  Until that
extension is supplied, admissibility is treated as part of the
shell data rather than as an independently derived analytic theorem.

\paragraph{NC-5. Classical-barrier framing.}
The Six Birds approach shares the spirit of the classical RH attack
programs but is structurally distinct from them: Hilbert--P\'olya's
spectral framing \citep{BerryKeating1990,Odlyzko1987}, Connes'
adelic and noncommutative-geometric framing
\citep{Connes1999,Connes2016}, de Branges' Hilbert spaces of entire
functions \citep{deBranges1968,deBranges1986,deBranges1994},
Beurling--Nyman positivity
\citep{Nyman1950,Beurling1955,BaezDuarte2003}, and Weil's
number-theoretic positivity \citep{Weil1952,Bombieri2000} all aim at
unconditional RH within their respective classical substrates.  The
present paper instead establishes a conditional result on the typed
Selberg-class trace shell, with the closure-content recognition
source supplied separately.  The classical programs are neither
circumvented nor refuted; they address a different mathematical
object.  In particular, Weil positivity is readout-level and
target-adjacent in the V-Differential classification rather than the
source-level recognition content used here.  Acceptance of the
present theorem turns on the stated recognition source rather than
on a claimed resolution of any classical program.

\paragraph{NC-6. GRH is out of scope.}
Generalizing to the Riemann hypothesis for primitive Selberg-class
\(L\)-functions \citep{Selberg1992,KaczorowskiPerelli1999} is an
extension target.  The SDTC structural law would need to be
instantiated for each \(L\) of interest, with a separate analysis
of typed-shell admissibility and recognition-source provenance for
that \(L\).  This paper instantiates the law for \(L=\zeta\) only;
GRH is out of scope.  No assertion is made here about
Dirichlet \citep{Davenport2000}, modular \citep{Iwaniec1997},
automorphic \citep{CogdellKimMurty2004}, or other primitive
Selberg-class \(L\)-functions.

\paragraph{NC-7. Simple zeros and density results.}
No claim is made about multiplicity one for nontrivial zeros, and
no zero-density claim is made.  The typed multiset \(\Znt\) admits
arbitrary positive integer multiplicities \(m_{\rho}>0\); the
conditional landing chain delivers the critical-line statement on
the typed zero ledger, while simple-zero conjectures
\citep{Montgomery1973,ConreyGhoshGonek1998}, Lindel\"of-type
statements \citep{TitchmarshHeathBrown1986,Ivic1985}, and
zero-density refinements \citep{Montgomery1971,Ivic1985} are
distinct questions with their own structural status.

\paragraph{NC-8. Three-route derivation sweep.}
The recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is not derivable from
framework primitives.  The three candidate primitive paths---the
framework's primitive role inventory, the audited interaction
calculus, and the closure-formation discipline---were each found
insufficient to produce the trace-vanishing domination sequence.
This marks the epistemic status of the source as closure content
rather than a primitive theorem.

\paragraph{NC-9. No-smuggling gates.}
The conditional closure passes the six no-smuggling audit gates
plus Gate 7, the recognition-source provenance audit.
Appendix~\ref{app:formalization} records the seven checks alongside
the Foundations-III audited judgment vocabulary
\cite{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026}.

\paragraph{NC-10. Scope of the realisability-instance form.}
The realisability-instance form
\(\Sel\in\mathrm{RefStableAOR}^{\mathrm{ref}}_{S_{\mathrm{RH}}}\)
delivered by \Cref{thm:rh:conditional,thm:rh:aor-instance} is a
statement about the typed zero ledger of \(\Sel\) under
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\); the discharge atoms of the eight-stratum /
twelve-type residual atlas are assembled literally from the shell,
the recognition source, and the bridge fields, so the
classification carries the same conditional content as
\(\GamSDTCSelberg \Rightarrow \mathrm{RH}\) and inherits every
preceding scope boundary unchanged.

\paragraph{NC-11. Realisability is the readout's stratum, not a
new hypothesis discharge.}
The audit-closure rendering does not remove or substitute for any
load-bearing hypothesis of the conditional landing chain.
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\) and Foundations-II admissibility of \(\Sel\)
remain the supplied structural content; the realisability-instance
form names the audit-closure stratum at which the conditional
landing chain delivers its conclusion, with the critical-line
statement on \(\Znt\) as its typed-zero-ledger readout.  No
unconditional RH claim in standard ZFC follows from the
realisability framing, and no classical analytic input is claimed to
be replaced or strengthened by it.

These boundaries are part of the conditional claim, not retreats
from it.  The recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is the
structural content supplied by \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}, and the
conditional landing chain converts that supplied content into a
critical-line statement on the typed zero ledger of \(\Sel\).  The
boundaries above identify what is supplied, what is encoded, and
what remains an open extension.
