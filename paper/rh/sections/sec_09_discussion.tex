\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The conditional landing chain invites three lines of further thought:
where it sits in the broader Six Birds program of formed-layer
structural laws, how it should be compared with classical approaches
to RH, and which extensions remain open after the present
formalization.

\paragraph{Structural parallels.}
The structural template used here is not isolated.  It has the same
shape as two sibling Six Birds layer-level results: the no-needles
closure of incompressible Navier--Stokes regularity and the
CSL-SAT-hiddenness closure of the PvNP problem.  In all three cases
the substrate is first organized as a formed closure; a recognition
source names the structural content supplied by that closure; and an
apparatus theorem extracts the advertised mathematical consequence
once that content is present.  The sibling results are mentioned here
only as structural parallels, not as inputs to the RH argument.

The SDTC structural law used in this paper is the involutive
self-dual member of that family \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}.  Its
particular apparatus is the duality-confinement membrane theorem:
given a separating anti-invariant readout and a vanishing trace
domination sequence, the anti-invariant ledger collapses.  In the RH
instance, the recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) supplies the
domination records for \(\AZ\), and the bridge theorem in
\Cref{thm:rh:dc-master-applied} applies the Paper 1 master theorem to
\(\Sel\).  Thus the present paper is the worked single-substrate
validation of SDTC on the saturated Selberg trace shell, just as the
sibling tracks are worked validations of their own formed-layer
structural laws.  The unconditional analytic content of any one of
these tracks is not derived from a common primitive calculus; it is
closure content of its formed layer, supplied as recognition source.
This is why the parallels are useful but not load-bearing.  They show
that the paper is following a stable formed-layer pattern inside the
Six Birds program, while the actual RH argument still depends only on
the RH-specific shell, the Paper 1 SDTC apparatus, and the
Selberg-class recognition source.

\paragraph{Classical RH programs.}
The comparison with classical RH approaches should be made at the
right level.  The present paper does not circumvent or refute the
classical programs.  Hilbert--Pólya's spectral framing seeks an
unconditional spectral interpretation of the zero set.  Connes'
adelic and noncommutative-geometric framing seeks an unconditional
trace-formula interpretation in a different geometric substrate.  De
Branges' Hilbert spaces of entire functions program seeks an
unconditional positivity mechanism in its own functional-analytic
setting.  Beurling--Nyman seeks an unconditional density criterion in
\(L^{p}\)-type spaces.  Weil's number-theoretic positivity framing
seeks unconditional positivity through the explicit formula.  These
are all programs aimed at unconditional RH inside classical
mathematical substrates.

The conditional theorem here lives at a different layer.  Its
substrate is the typed saturated trace shell \(\Sel\), and its
load-bearing hypothesis is \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), the recognition source
that supplies domination records as closure content.  That distinction
is sharpest in the comparison with Weil positivity.  Weil-style
positivity is close in spirit to the target: it sees the
explicit-formula positivity phenomena that a successful RH argument
must control.  In the V-Differential classification, however, this is
readout-level or target-adjacent content.  It does not by itself
become the source-level content required by the duality-confinement
mechanism, namely the formed-layer existence of completed domination
records \(\AZ\preceq B_n\) with \(\trace B_n\to0\).  The development
history records this audit-currency tension: classical positivity
content may illuminate the right trace quantities, but it does not
supply the recognition source used by the conditional landing chain.

This distinction is the central comparison point for an
outside-Six-Birds reader.  The paper is not claiming that the classical programs
failed and that the present method bypasses them.  It is claiming a
conditional theorem on a different object: the typed Selberg-class
trace shell under the standard Six Birds closure assumption and the
recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\).  Accepting or rejecting the
result therefore turns on whether one accepts that formed-layer
recognition source as the correct structural input, not on whether
one accepts a claimed classical proof of Weil positivity, a spectral
operator, or a de Branges-space positivity theorem.
Put differently, the paper changes the question being certified.  It
does not certify an unconditional analytic construction of all zeros
on the critical line.  It certifies that, once the saturated Selberg
trace shell is admitted and the SDTC recognition source is supplied,
the landing chain from domination records to \(\AZ=0\) to
\(\Real(\rho)=1/2\) is direct.  This is exactly the conditional
reading stated in \Cref{sec:landing_chain,sec:scope_and_nonclaims}.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Three extension targets are natural.  First, the admissibility of
\(\Sel\) should be derived rather than carried as construction-time
audit data.  In the present paper, \(\Sel\) contains an audit field
recording admissibility with respect to the seven Foundations-II
schemas.  A stronger formal development would unfold those schemas as
predicates and verify them for the completed Selberg trace instance.
This would replace a construction-time hypothesis by a derived
formed-layer admissibility theorem.
Such a derivation would not change Theorem T or the conditional
landing chain; it would strengthen the construction of the shell on
which those statements are evaluated.

Second, the real-coordinate parameter identification should be
formalized at the analytic level.  The current mathlib-free encoding
uses a typed real-coordinate carrier, with the identification to the
classical \(\Real(\rho)\) made at shell-construction time.  A stronger
development would connect the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) and its
real-coordinate field to the actual nontrivial zeros of \(\zeta\) by
analytic-number-theoretic infrastructure.  This would lift the
real-part abstraction boundary disclosed in
\Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger}.
This is the extension most directly relevant to readers who want a
fully classical interpretation of the typed zero ledger inside the
formal system itself.

Third, the natural generalization is to primitive Selberg-class
\(L\)-functions.  The functional-equation involution, completed
\(L\)-function, nontrivial-zero ledger, and anti-invariant ledger all
have evident analogues for a primitive \(L\).  But the analogy is not
itself a theorem.  Each \(L\) would need its own typed shell, its own
admissibility verification, and its own recognition-source provenance
argument.  The resulting statement would be a per-\(L\) conditional
landing chain for the corresponding generalized Riemann hypothesis,
not an automatic GRH theorem obtained by changing notation.
The recognition-source question would also repeat for each \(L\):
the formal similarity of the ledgers does not by itself provide the
domination records.

Among these, the admissibility derivation and the real-coordinate
identification are closest to the existing formalization.  The GRH
extension is structurally specified by the SDTC pattern, but its
substantive realization is a per-\(L\) development problem.  The
present paper should therefore be read as a worked single-substrate
validation for \(L=\zeta\), not as the final form of the broader
RH-for-\(L\) program.
