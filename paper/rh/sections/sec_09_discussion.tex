\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The conditional landing chain invites three lines of further thought:
where it sits in the broader Six Birds program of formed-layer
structural laws, how it should be compared with classical approaches
to RH, and which extensions remain open after the present
formalization.

\paragraph{Structural parallels.}
The structural template used here is not isolated.  It has the same
shape as two further Six Birds layer-level results: the no-needles
closure of incompressible Navier--Stokes regularity and the
CSL-SAT-hiddenness closure of the PvNP problem.  In all three cases
the substrate is first organized as a formed closure; a recognition
source names the structural content supplied by that closure; and
an apparatus theorem extracts the advertised mathematical
consequence once that content is present.  Those results are
mentioned here only as structural parallels, not as inputs to the
RH argument.

The SDTC structural law used in this paper is the involutive
self-dual member of that family \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}.  Its
particular apparatus is the duality-confinement membrane theorem:
given a separating anti-invariant readout and a vanishing trace
domination sequence, the anti-invariant ledger collapses.  In the
RH instance, the recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) supplies the
domination records for \(\AZ\), and the bridge theorem in
\Cref{thm:rh:dc-master-applied} applies the
\cite{TsiokosSDTC2026} master theorem to \(\Sel\).  Thus the
present paper is the worked single-substrate validation of SDTC on
the saturated Selberg trace shell, just as the no-needles and
CSL-SAT-hiddenness developments are worked validations of their
own formed-layer structural laws.  The unconditional analytic content of any one of
these tracks is not derived from a common primitive calculus; it is
closure content of its formed layer, supplied as recognition source.
This is why the parallels are useful but not load-bearing.  They show
that the paper is following a stable formed-layer pattern inside the
Six Birds program, while the actual RH argument still depends only on
the RH-specific shell, the SDTC apparatus of \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026},
and the Selberg-class recognition source.

\paragraph{Classical RH programs.}
The comparison with classical RH approaches should be made at the
right level.  The present paper does not circumvent or refute the
classical programs.  Hilbert--Pólya's spectral framing seeks an
unconditional spectral interpretation of the zero set.  Connes'
adelic and noncommutative-geometric framing seeks an unconditional
trace-formula interpretation in a different geometric substrate.  De
Branges' Hilbert spaces of entire functions program seeks an
unconditional positivity mechanism in its own functional-analytic
setting.  Beurling--Nyman seeks an unconditional density criterion in
\(L^{p}\)-type spaces.  Weil's number-theoretic positivity framing
seeks unconditional positivity through the explicit formula.  These
are all programs aimed at unconditional RH inside classical
mathematical substrates.

The conditional theorem here lives at a different layer.  Its
substrate is the typed saturated trace shell \(\Sel\), and its
load-bearing hypothesis is \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), the recognition source
that supplies domination records as closure content.  That distinction
is sharpest in the comparison with Weil positivity.  Weil-style
positivity is close in spirit to the target: it sees the
explicit-formula positivity phenomena that a successful RH argument
must control.  In the V-Differential classification, however, this is
readout-level or target-adjacent content.  It does not by itself
become the source-level content required by the duality-confinement
mechanism, namely the formed-layer existence of completed domination
records \(\AZ\preceq B_n\) with \(\trace B_n\to0\).  Classical
positivity content may illuminate the right trace quantities, but it
does not by itself supply the recognition source used by the
conditional landing chain.

For an outside-Six-Birds reader, the comparison point is therefore
not whether the classical programs have failed and the present
method bypasses them.  It is that the paper proves a conditional
theorem about a different object: the typed Selberg-class trace
shell under the standard Six Birds closure assumption and the
recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\).  Acceptance turns on whether
one accepts that formed-layer recognition source as the correct
structural input, not on a claimed classical proof of Weil
positivity, a spectral operator, or a de Branges-space positivity
theorem.

\paragraph{Realisability stratum of the conclusion.}
The audit-closure framing of \citet{TsiokosAOR2026} fixes the
stratum at which the conditional landing chain delivers its
conclusion.  The eight-stratum / twelve-type residual atlas of the
AOR meta-theory partitions every audit obligation that a formed-layer
closure must discharge to count as a refinement-stable realisability
instance; in the present setting the seven discharge atoms named in
\Cref{def:rh:aor-sel-instance} are precisely the audit obligations
incurred by the recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), the bridge
theorem \Cref{thm:rh:dc-master-applied}, and the translation theorem
T, and the AOR-instance theorem records that no residue remains
outside the deferred-by-construction discharges named there.  The
realisability-instance form is therefore not a separate result
about \(\Sel\) but the audit-stratum certificate of the same
conditional landing chain; the critical-line statement on \(\Znt\)
is its typed-zero-ledger readout.  Within the Lean development this
certificate is recorded as partial because its Lean conclusion is
the local syntactic membership predicate, not the full audit-closure
characterisation.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Three extension targets are natural.  First, the admissibility of
\(\Sel\) should be derived rather than carried as construction-time
audit data.  A stronger development would unfold the seven
Foundations-II schemas as predicates and verify them for the
completed Selberg trace instance, replacing a construction-time
hypothesis by a derived formed-layer admissibility theorem; this
would not change Theorem T or the conditional landing chain.

Second, the real-coordinate parameter identification should be
formalized at the analytic level.  A stronger development would
connect the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) and its real-coordinate
field to the nontrivial zeros of \(\zeta\) by analytic-number-theoretic
infrastructure, lifting the real-part abstraction boundary disclosed
in \Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger}.

Third, the natural generalization is to primitive Selberg-class
\(L\)-functions.  The functional-equation involution, completed
\(L\)-function, nontrivial-zero ledger, and anti-invariant ledger
all have evident analogues, but the analogy is not itself a
theorem: each \(L\) needs its own typed shell, admissibility
verification, and recognition-source provenance argument.  The
resulting statement is a per-\(L\) conditional landing chain for the
corresponding generalized Riemann hypothesis, not an automatic GRH
theorem obtained by changing notation.

The admissibility derivation and the real-coordinate identification
are closest to the existing formalization; the GRH extension is
structurally specified by the SDTC pattern, but its substantive
realization is a per-\(L\) development problem.
