\section{Involution and ledger}
\label{sec:involution_and_ledger}

The first ingredients of the RH closure are the
functional-equation involution and its anti-invariant readout.  Both
are elementary objects on the complex plane.  Their role in the
structural argument is not to add analytic depth, but to place the
critical line inside the involutive-ledger pattern described in
\Cref{sec:framework}: an involution, a fixed locus, and a scalar
quantity that changes sign under the involution.  This is the point at
which the ordinary functional-equation symmetry of the completed zeta
function becomes a typed datum of the closure.

\begin{definition}[Functional-equation involution \(\JL\)]
\label{def:rh:fe-involution}
The functional-equation involution on the complex plane is
\[
  \JL(s) = 1-\bar{s}.
\]
Equivalently, if \(s=x+iy\), then
\[
  \JL(s) = 1-x+iy.
\]
Thus \(\JL\) is reflection across the vertical line
\(\Real(s)=1/2\).
\end{definition}

This definition uses only the affine symmetry \(s\mapsto 1-\bar{s}\).
No assertion about which points are zeros is being made yet.  The
purpose is to isolate the geometric symmetry that any zero ledger will
inherit once the completed zeta substrate is placed in the saturated
trace shell.  Points on opposite sides of the critical line are
interchanged; points on the critical line are unchanged.

The map \(\JL\) is an involution by direct calculation:
\[
  \JL(\JL(s))
    = \JL(1-\bar{s})
    = 1-\overline{1-\bar{s}}
    = 1-(1-s)
    = s.
\]
Hence \(\JL^{2}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{C}}\).  The same coordinate formula gives
the fixed locus.  Writing \(s=x+iy\), the equation \(\JL(s)=s\) is
\[
  1-x+iy = x+iy,
\]
which is equivalent to \(x=1/2\).  Therefore
\[
  \Fix(\JL)
    =
  \{\,s\in\mathbb{C} : \Real(s)=1/2\,\},
\]
the critical line.  Conversely, any point with real part \(1/2\) is
unchanged by \(\JL\), so the displayed set is exactly the fixed locus.
Thus the critical-line condition is equivalently the statement that a
point is fixed by the functional-equation involution.  This is the
form needed later: the RH assertion on the typed zero ledger will be
read as the statement that every ledger point lies in \(\Fix(\JL)\).

\begin{definition}[Anti-invariant readout \(\psimRH\)]
\label{def:rh:psi-minus-rh}
The anti-invariant readout associated with \(\JL\) is the signed
horizontal displacement from the critical line,
\[
  \psimRH(s)=\Real(s)-\frac{1}{2}.
\]
\end{definition}

The word ``signed'' is important.  The absolute distance to the
critical line would lose the side of the line on which \(s\) lies,
whereas the structural argument needs a quantity that reverses sign
under the involution.  The readout \(\psimRH\) is positive to the
right of the critical line, negative to the left, and zero exactly on
the line.  It is therefore a scalar witness for the failure of a point
to be fixed by \(\JL\).

This readout separates the two sides of the critical line and vanishes
exactly on \(\Fix(\JL)\).  Indeed, \(\psimRH(s)=0\) is precisely
\(\Real(s)=1/2\), which is the fixed-locus condition above.  It is also
anti-invariant under \(\JL\).  Since
\(\Real(1-\bar{s})=1-\Real(s)\), one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \psimRH(\JL(s))
    &= \Real(1-\bar{s})-\frac{1}{2}  \\
    &= 1-\Real(s)-\frac{1}{2}        \\
    &= \frac{1}{2}-\Real(s)          \\
    &= -\bigl(\Real(s)-\frac{1}{2}\bigr) \\
    &= -\psimRH(s).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the readout detects the anti-invariant component of a point with
respect to reflection across the critical line.

This is the concrete RH specialization of the abstract separating
readout used in the duality-confinement apparatus.  On the RH side the
response is one-dimensional and real-valued: all information retained
by this readout is the signed real-part displacement from \(1/2\).
Consequently, forcing the squared aggregate of this readout to vanish
will be enough to force each zero in the ledger onto the fixed locus,
provided the ledger has positive multiplicities.  That aggregate is
introduced after the zero ledger itself is in place.

The formalization appendix records these two definitions as
definition entries for the RH axis.  The accompanying involutivity,
fixed-locus characterization, vanishing condition, and anti-invariance
calculation are checked in Lean with faithful semantic alignment to the
statements just displayed.

With the involution and readout in place, the remaining objects are
the typed multiset of nontrivial zeros and the scalar built from it.

\begin{definition}[Nontrivial-zero ledger]
\label{def:rh:nontrivial-zero-ledger}
Let
\[
  \Lzeta(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)
\]
denote the completed Riemann zeta function.  The nontrivial-zero
ledger of \(\Lzeta\) is the typed multiset
\[
  \Znt
    =
  \bigl\{\,(\rho,m_{\rho}) :
      \rho\in\mathbb{C},
      \Lzeta(\rho)=0,
      0<\Real(\rho)<1,
      m_{\rho}>0
  \,\bigr\}_{\mathrm{multi}},
\]
where \(m_{\rho}\) is the positive integer multiplicity of \(\rho\)
as a zero of \(\Lzeta\).  The associated atomic ledger measure is
\(\mu_{L}(\{\rho\})=m_{\rho}\).
\end{definition}

Plainly, \(\Znt\) is a multiset rather than a set: if a zero occurs
with multiplicity \(m_{\rho}\), the ledger records that multiplicity
as part of the data.  The completed function \(\Lzeta\) has the same
zeros in the critical strip as the Riemann zeta function; the trivial
zeros at the negative even integers are outside this ledger, and in
the completed expression they are removed from the zero side by the
completion factor.  The present paper makes no simplicity assumption.
Even though one often expects nontrivial zeros to be simple, all
arguments below allow arbitrary positive integer multiplicities.

\begin{definition}[Anti-invariant zero ledger]
\label{def:rh:anti-invariant-zero-ledger}
The anti-invariant zero ledger of \(\zeta\) is the
multiplicity-weighted scalar
\[
  \AZ
    =
  \sum_{\rho\in\Znt}
    m_{\rho}\,
    \left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}.
\]
Equivalently, using the readout from
\Cref{def:rh:psi-minus-rh},
\[
  \AZ
    =
  \sum_{\rho\in\Znt}
    m_{\rho}\,\left|\psimRH(\rho)\right|^{2},
\]
with summation over the typed multiset \(\Znt\), hence with
multiplicity.
\end{definition}

Each summand \(m_{\rho}|\psimRH(\rho)|^{2}\) is nonnegative: the
multiplicity is positive by definition, and the squared real-part
displacement is nonnegative.  Therefore \(\AZ\) is a nonnegative
scalar.  On a finite typed multiset, \(\AZ=0\) can occur only if every
summand vanishes; because \(m_{\rho}>0\), this is equivalent to
\(\psimRH(\rho)=0\) for every typed zero \(\rho\).  By the fixed-locus
calculation above, this is exactly the assertion that every typed zero
lies on the critical line.  \Cref{sec:translation_theorem} packages
this positivity argument as Theorem T.

One encoding disclosure belongs here.  In the formalization, the
real-part coordinate is parameterized by a typed real-coordinate
carrier rather than by the standard real numbers with their full
analytic infrastructure.  The identification of this typed
coordinate with the \(\mathbb{R}\)-valued real part of a classical
nontrivial zero of \(\zeta\) is fixed by construction-parameter
assignment when the saturated Selberg trace shell is instantiated;
it is not derived as an internal Lean predicate.  The mathlib-free
constraint forbids importing the analytic \(\mathbb{R}\)-typed
machinery needed for such a derivation, and a substantive Lean
identification is left as an extension.  The mathematical content is
unchanged: RH on the typed zero ledger says that every such
real-part coordinate equals \(1/2\), and the user-supplied
assignment makes that typed statement equivalent to the classical
RH statement.

The saturated completed Selberg trace closure \(\Sel\), which
packages these typed objects together with the trace instrument and
audit data, is the subject of \Cref{sec:sat_sel_shell}.
