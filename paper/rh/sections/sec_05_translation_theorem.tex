\section{Translation theorem T}
\label{sec:translation_theorem}

With the saturated trace shell \(\Sel\) in hand, the next step is to
translate the typed-ledger statement \(\AZ=0\) into the
number-theoretic critical-line statement.  This translation is
elementary.  It uses only the definition of \(\AZ\) as a
multiplicity-weighted sum of squared signed displacements from the
critical line.  Its importance is structural: the
duality-confinement mechanism will later deliver \(\AZ=0\), and
translation theorem T is the bridge that turns this ledger vanishing
into the RH statement on the typed zero ledger.

The theorem is stated on the zero ledger carried by \(\Sel\).  The
real-part coordinate is the typed coordinate fixed in
\Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger}; under the construction-parameter
assignment described there, this typed statement is the corresponding
classical assertion about the real parts of nontrivial zeros of
\(\zeta\).

\begin{theorem}[Translation theorem T]
\label{thm:rh:translation-T}
On the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) of the saturated completed Selberg
trace closure \(\Sel\),
\[
  \AZ=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \forall\,\rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}.
\]
Equivalently, the anti-invariant zero ledger vanishes if and only if
every typed zero lies on the critical line \(\Fix(\JL)\).
\end{theorem}

The plain mathematical content is a direct positivity argument.  For
each typed zero \(\rho\), the summand
\[
  m_{\rho}\left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}
  =
  m_{\rho}\left|\psimRH(\rho)\right|^{2}
\]
is nonnegative, and the multiplicity \(m_{\rho}\) is positive by the
definition of \(\Znt\).  Thus a zero value of \(\AZ\) can only occur
when every squared displacement is zero.  Conversely, if every typed
zero has \(\Real(\rho)=1/2\), then every displacement term is zero and
the entire sum defining \(\AZ\) is zero.  The forward and reverse
directions are short enough to be written out explicitly; they are the
content of the next pass of this section.

This theorem is what makes the conditional landing chain land on RH
rather than on an internal trace statement.  The trace-side output of
the later argument is \(\AZ=0\).  Theorem T converts that output into
the assertion that every \(\rho\in\Znt\) satisfies
\(\Real(\rho)=1/2\), which is precisely RH on the typed zero ledger of
\(\Sel\).  No recognition-source content is used in this translation:
Theorem T depends only on \(\Znt\), the positive multiplicities
\(m_{\rho}\), the readout \(\psimRH\), and the definition of \(\AZ\).

Theorem T is therefore a translation theorem rather than the place
where the conditional content enters: it does not assume RH, does
not use the recognition source, and does not invoke a trace-formula
positivity theorem.  Its positivity is the elementary positivity of
a finite multiplicity-weighted sum of squares.  The later sections
supply the structural reason for \(\AZ=0\) under the conditional
hypothesis; once \(\AZ=0\) is available, the theorem above contains
the entire conversion to the right-hand side.

Both halves admit short positivity-based proofs; Lean verifies each
half with faithful semantic alignment.  As recorded in the
formalization appendix, the Lean proof composes the
\texttt{AntiInvariantZeroLedger} structure fields that supply the
multiplicity-weighted-sum positivity content (vanishing summand from
vanishing sum, vanishing displacement from vanishing summand, and
their converses); the underlying finite-sum and scalar arithmetic
are part of the typed interface rather than derivations from lower
arithmetic primitives.

\begin{theorem}[Translation theorem T, forward direction]
\label{thm:rh:translation-T-forward}
On the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) of \(\Sel\), if \(\AZ=0\), then
\[
  \forall\,\rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By definition,
\[
  \AZ
    =
  \sum_{\rho\in\Znt}
    m_{\rho}\left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}.
\]
For every typed zero \(\rho\), the multiplicity satisfies
\(m_{\rho}>0\), and the square
\(\left|\Real(\rho)-1/2\right|^{2}\) is nonnegative.  Hence every
summand is nonnegative.  If \(\AZ=0\), then the finite sum of these
nonnegative summands is zero, so each summand must be zero.  Therefore
\[
  m_{\rho}\left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}=0
\]
for each \(\rho\in\Znt\).  Since \(m_{\rho}>0\), the squared factor
must be zero.  Thus \(\Real(\rho)-1/2=0\), and hence
\(\Real(\rho)=1/2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Translation theorem T, reverse direction]
\label{thm:rh:translation-T-reverse}
On the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) of \(\Sel\), if
\[
  \forall\,\rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\frac{1}{2},
\]
then \(\AZ=0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume that every \(\rho\in\Znt\) satisfies \(\Real(\rho)=1/2\).
Then
\[
  \Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}=0
\]
for every typed zero, and therefore
\[
  \left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}=0.
\]
Each summand in the definition of \(\AZ\) is consequently
\[
  m_{\rho}\left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}
    =
  m_{\rho}\cdot 0
    =
  0.
\]
Thus \(\AZ\) is a finite sum of zero summands, so \(\AZ=0\).
\end{proof}

Together, the two halves give the biconditional of
\Cref{thm:rh:translation-T}: \(\AZ=0\) if and only if every typed
zero lies on the critical line.  The remaining structural task is
to supply the recognition source that delivers \(\AZ=0\) through
the duality-confinement mechanism, which is the subject of
\Cref{sec:recognition_source} and \Cref{sec:landing_chain}.
