\section{Translation theorem T}
\label{sec:translation_theorem}

With the saturated trace shell \(\Sel\) in hand, the next step is to
translate the typed-ledger statement \(\AZ=0\) into the
number-theoretic critical-line statement.  The translation is
elementary, using only the definition of \(\AZ\) as a
multiplicity-weighted sum of squared signed displacements from the
critical line.  Its role is structural: the duality-confinement
mechanism will later deliver \(\AZ=0\), and translation theorem T is
the bridge that turns this ledger vanishing into the RH statement on
the typed zero ledger.

The theorem is stated on the zero ledger carried by \(\Sel\).  The
real-part coordinate is the typed coordinate fixed in
\Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger}; under the construction-parameter
assignment described there, the typed statement is the corresponding
classical assertion about the real parts of nontrivial zeros of
\(\zeta\).

\begin{theorem}[Translation theorem T\footnote{Checked in the Lean formalization by typed-interface composition over the anti-invariant-zero-ledger fields; see App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{thm:rh:translation-T}
On the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) of the saturated completed Selberg
trace closure \(\Sel\),
\[
  \AZ=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \forall\,\rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}.
\]
Equivalently, the anti-invariant zero ledger vanishes if and only if
every typed zero lies on the critical line \(\Fix(\JL)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine
\Cref{thm:rh:translation-T-forward,thm:rh:translation-T-reverse},
which give the two directions of the biconditional.
\end{proof}

Theorem T is what makes the conditional landing chain land on RH
rather than on an internal trace statement.  The trace-side output
of the later argument is \(\AZ=0\); Theorem T converts that output
into the assertion that every \(\rho\in\Znt\) satisfies
\(\Real(\rho)=1/2\), which is RH on the typed zero ledger of
\(\Sel\).  No recognition-source content is used in this
translation: T depends only on \(\Znt\), the positive multiplicities
\(m_{\rho}\), the readout \(\psimRH\), and the definition of
\(\AZ\), and its positivity is the elementary positivity of a finite
multiplicity-weighted sum of squares.  The recognition source enters
later, as the conditional reason for \(\AZ=0\); once that vanishing
is available, the theorem completes the conversion to the
critical-line statement.

The two halves below are short positivity arguments.

\begin{theorem}[Translation theorem T, forward direction\footnote{Checked in the Lean formalization by typed-interface composition over the anti-invariant-zero-ledger fields; see App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{thm:rh:translation-T-forward}
On the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) of \(\Sel\), if \(\AZ=0\), then
\[
  \forall\,\rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By definition,
\[
  \AZ
    =
  \sum_{\rho\in\Znt}
    m_{\rho}\left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}.
\]
For every typed zero \(\rho\), the multiplicity satisfies
\(m_{\rho}>0\) and the square \(\left|\Real(\rho)-1/2\right|^{2}\)
is nonnegative, so every summand is nonnegative.  If \(\AZ=0\), the
finite sum of these nonnegative summands is zero, so each summand
is zero:
\[
  m_{\rho}\left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}=0
\]
for each \(\rho\in\Znt\).  Since \(m_{\rho}>0\), the squared factor
must vanish.  Thus \(\Real(\rho)-1/2=0\), and hence
\(\Real(\rho)=1/2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Translation theorem T, reverse direction\footnote{Checked in the Lean formalization by typed-interface composition over the anti-invariant-zero-ledger fields; see App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{thm:rh:translation-T-reverse}
On the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) of \(\Sel\), if
\[
  \forall\,\rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\frac{1}{2},
\]
then \(\AZ=0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume that every \(\rho\in\Znt\) satisfies \(\Real(\rho)=1/2\).
Then
\[
  \Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}=0
\]
for every typed zero, and therefore
\[
  \left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}=0.
\]
Each summand in the definition of \(\AZ\) is consequently
\[
  m_{\rho}\left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}
    =
  m_{\rho}\cdot 0
    =
  0.
\]
Thus \(\AZ\) is a finite sum of zero summands, so \(\AZ=0\).
\end{proof}

Together, the two halves give the biconditional of
\Cref{thm:rh:translation-T}: \(\AZ=0\) if and only if every typed
zero lies on the critical line.  The remaining structural task is
to supply the recognition source that delivers \(\AZ=0\) through
the duality-confinement mechanism; this is the subject of
\Cref{sec:recognition_source,sec:landing_chain}.
