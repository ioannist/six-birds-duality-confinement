\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

The framework context for this paper is the Six Birds organization of
formed-layer structural laws, specialized to the Riemann-zeta
substrate.  Six Birds Theory is used here as a typed and audited
calculus for closure formation in mathematical structures.  One
starts with a substrate---here the completed Riemann zeta function
together with its trace-relevant data---and records the carriers,
observables, comparison maps, quotient maps, and audit information
visible at the layer being studied.  When this typed record is
declared admissibly closed under the framework's formation
discipline, we call it a formed closure.  The present paper's formed
closure is the saturated completed Selberg trace closure \(\Sel\),
defined in \Cref{sec:sat_sel_shell}: the typed shell on which the
zero ledger, trace instrument, functional-equation involution, and
audit data all live.

The admissibility and recordkeeping vocabulary are inherited from the
Six Birds foundations.  Foundations II supplies the schema-level
discipline used to state that a typed shell carries the required
admissibility data, including the lawful trace instrument and the
visibility bookkeeping attached to it \cite{TsiokosFoundationsII2026}.
Foundations III supplies the audited judgment vocabulary used later
when the conditional landing chain is rendered as a framework-relative
claim \cite{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026}.  The underlying point is
simpler than the terminology: the framework does not replace the
classical zeta function, its functional equation, or the zero set.
It fixes a typed layer at which those data are organized and states
which structural facts are treated as available at that layer.

Throughout the paper, closure content is treated as a structural
fact of the formed layer rather than something that must be
re-derived from lower framework primitives.  A recognition source
is the name for such supplied content: an explicit hypothesis about
the substrate, with typed fields recording the data it supplies.
For this paper the recognition source is \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), which
supplies the domination-record sequence on \(\Sel\) that the
duality-confinement mechanism consumes.  Translation theorem T
(\Cref{sec:translation_theorem}) is an ordinary theorem about
\(\AZ\): it says that \(\AZ=0\) is equivalent to the typed RH
statement.  \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), by contrast, is not proved here;
it is the named structural source whose presence makes the
conditional landing chain run.

The V-Differential classification organizes such structural laws by
the determining-state information the formed layer may expose.  A
determining state is the internal state through which observable
behavior is required to factor; in a trace-state-only column, the
layer may expose trace observables on a typed positive cone but not
a richer witness that would solve the target problem directly.  For
the RH closure, the visible currency is \(\AZ\), the order
\(\preceq\), and the trace functional \(\trace\); the admissible
content has the form of trace domination records
\[
  \AZ \preceq B_n,
  \qquad
  \trace B_n \to 0,
\]
not a hidden list of zero locations.  \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}
identifies Self-Dual Trace Confinement, \(\GamSDTC\), as the
V-Differential trace-state-only law for involutive self-dual formed
closures; the present paper is the worked single-substrate
validation of that law for \(\Sel\).

Within the broader Six Birds program, SDTC sits beside parallel
internal developments that address other formed-layer closures---in
particular the incompressible Navier--Stokes regularity closure and
the PvNP closure---using the same architecture: a recognition source
supplies structural content of the formed layer, and an apparatus
theorem extracts the advertised mathematical consequence once that
content is present.  These developments are mentioned only to
position SDTC inside the program; none is an input to the RH
construction in this paper, and no result from them is used below.

The cross-paper dependency is one-way.  \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}
develops the abstract apparatus---the involutive object ledger, the
separating anti-invariant readout, the typed positive cone,
completed domination records, and the duality-confinement membrane
theorem.  The present paper imports that development substantively
for three items: the master theorem (reproduced where invoked in
\Cref{sec:landing_chain}), the SDTC structural-law framing, and the
V-Differential trace-state-only placement of the RH instance.  The
duality-confinement paper is self-contained and does not depend on
results derived here.

With this framework in place, the next sections stay close to the
mathematics.  \Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger} defines the
functional-equation involution \(\JL(s)=1-\bar{s}\), the
anti-invariant readout \(\psimRH(s)=\Real(s)-1/2\), the typed
nontrivial-zero ledger \(\Znt\), and the scalar ledger \(\AZ\).
\Cref{sec:sat_sel_shell} packages these objects into \(\Sel\).
\Cref{sec:translation_theorem} proves that \(\AZ=0\) is exactly the
RH statement on the typed zero ledger, while
\Cref{sec:recognition_source} and \Cref{sec:landing_chain} explain
how \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) supplies the domination records and how the
master theorem converts them into \(\AZ=0\).  The framework
vocabulary in this section is just the organization needed to read
that chain: formed closure, closure content, trace-state-only
visibility, recognition source, and the one-way import from
\citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}.
