\section{Direct confinement}
\label{sec:direct_confinement}

With the trace identity in place, the separating-readout conditions
immediately give two confinement consequences.  The first is
qualitative: if the anti-invariant ledger vanishes, then visible
mass cannot remain away from the fixed locus.  The second is
quantitative: if the readout grows at least by a modulus away from
\(\Fix(J)\), then the trace of \(\AX\) bounds the amount of visible
mass at that distance scale.  These results are the consequence
step used later by the master theorem; the master theorem adds the
separate squeeze argument that forces \(\AX=0\).

\begin{theorem}[Separation--confinement]
\label{thm:duality_confinement:separation-confinement}
Let \(\IOL\) be an involutive object ledger with anti-invariant
ledger \(\AX\) and qualitatively separating anti-invariant readout
\(\psim\).  If \(\AX=0\) in the typed positive cone, then
\[
  \mu\bigl(X\setminus\Fix(J)\bigr)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{lem:duality_confinement:trace-identity},
\[
  \trace\AX=\int_X\|\psim(x)\|^2\,d\mu(x).
\]
Since \(\AX=0\), the left-hand side is zero.  The integrand on the
right is nonnegative, so the integral can vanish only when
\(\|\psim(x)\|=0\) for \(\mu\)-almost every visible point.  The
qualitative separation condition of
\Cref{def:duality_confinement:separating-readout} identifies the
zero set of \(\psim\) with \(\Fix(J)\) on the visible support.
Hence the complement of \(\Fix(J)\) has \(\mu\)-measure zero.
\end{proof}

In the finite weighted case, the same proof says that every visible
object with positive weight lies in \(\Fix(J)\).  Lean checks the
separation--confinement theorem as a typed-bridge composition: the
measure-theoretic reading of ``\(\psim\) vanishes
\(\mu\)-almost everywhere'' is supplied as two explicit hypotheses
on the typed cone, namely the implication
\(\mathtt{mu\_zero\_of\_ae}\) (\(\psim\)-vanishing a.e.\ to
\(\mu(X\setminus\Fix(J))=0\)) and the visible-support refinement
\(\mathtt{visible\_zero\_of\_ae}\) (\(\psim\)-vanishing a.e.\ to
pointwise vanishing on visible support).  Lean composes these
bridge hypotheses with the trace identity and separation; the
formalization appendix records the declaration-level
correspondence.

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative confinement]
\label{thm:duality_confinement:quantitative-confinement}
Let \(\IOL\) be an involutive object ledger with anti-invariant
ledger \(\AX\) and quantitatively separating anti-invariant readout
\(\psim\) with modulus \(m\).  Then, for every
\(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
  \mu\bigl(\{x\in X:\dist(x,\Fix(J))\geq\varepsilon\}\bigr)
  \leq
  \frac{\trace\AX}{m(\varepsilon)^2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(\varepsilon>0\), and set
\[
  E_\varepsilon=\{x\in X:\dist(x,\Fix(J))\geq\varepsilon\}.
\]
Quantitative separation gives
\[
  \|\psim(x)\|\geq m(\varepsilon)
  \qquad (x\in E_\varepsilon).
\]
Therefore
\[
  m(\varepsilon)^2\,\mu(E_\varepsilon)
  \leq
  \int_{E_\varepsilon}\|\psim(x)\|^2\,d\mu(x)
  \leq
  \int_X\|\psim(x)\|^2\,d\mu(x).
\]
The trace identity identifies the final integral with
\(\trace\AX\).  Since \(m(\varepsilon)>0\), division by
\(m(\varepsilon)^2\) gives the displayed bound.
\end{proof}

Lean records the quantitative-confinement bound via a typed
Markov interface: the Markov-style scalar step
(``\(m(\varepsilon)^2\,\mu(E_\varepsilon) \leq \trace\AX\) follows
from the integrand lower bound and the trace identity'') is
supplied as a typed hypothesis on the modulus side rather than
derived from an ordered-scalar primitive, and the theorem composes
it with the trace identity.  The formalization appendix records
the declaration-level correspondence.

\Cref{sec:master_theorem} will combine these direct consequences
with a domination squeeze: once a sequence of records
\(\AX\preceq B_n\) with \(\trace B_n\to0\) forces \(\AX=0\),
\Cref{thm:duality_confinement:separation-confinement} converts that
algebraic collapse into fixed-locus confinement.  The quantitative
bound above supplies the corresponding scale-sensitive refinement
when one works with finite-window or approximate data.
