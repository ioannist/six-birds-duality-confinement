\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

The framework context for SDTC is the Six Birds organization of
formed-layer structural laws.  In this paper, Six Birds Theory is
used as a typed and audited calculus for the closure-formation
discipline originating in Foundations I: it gives names to the
carriers, observables, comparison maps, and audit data that a
mathematical substrate presents when treated as a formed object.
A \emph{formed closure} is such a typed bundle together with the
assumption that the bundle has been closed under the relevant
formation discipline.  Concretely, one may think of a
substrate---algebraic, analytic, computational, or
geometric---equipped with a finite amount of visible structure and
with a declaration that this visible structure is stable under the
closure rules being used.  The admissibility schemas and typed
record interfaces used for this purpose are those of Foundations II
\cite{TsiokosFoundationsII2026}; the audited vocabulary of
visibility, threshold, gate, and claim-status data is part of the
Foundations III layer \cite{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026}.

This language does not replace the underlying mathematics; it
records which data are visible at a given layer and which comparison
principles are licensed there.  When the paper later writes an
involutive ledger \(\IOL\), a positive cone containing \(\AX\), or a
domination sequence \(B_n\), these objects do not add analytic
content.  They make explicit which carrier, readout, order relation,
trace functional, and audit conditions are in play.  The local
mathematics is the operator-style squeeze of the introduction; the
typed framework fixes the bookkeeping that keeps the squeeze inside
the same formed layer throughout.

Closure content is treated as a structural fact of the formed layer
rather than something to be re-derived from earlier framework
primitives.  If a witness, comparison map, domination sequence, or
recognition record is part of what makes the layer formed, the
structural law is allowed to name that item as supplied data.  This
is not an axiom inserted into the surrounding metatheory; it
identifies which hypothesis about the substrate is load-bearing.

The framework distinguishes a structural law from an apparatus
theorem.  The structural law names the formed-layer recognition
content; here \(\GamSDTC\) says that an admissibly formed involutive
self-dual closure supplies the trace-vanishing domination data
needed by the confinement mechanism.  The apparatus theorem is the
ordinary mathematical implication proved once those data are
present: a separating anti-invariant readout and bounds
\(\AX \preceq B_n\) with \(\trace B_n\to0\) force confinement on
\(\Fix(J)\).

The V-Differential classification is the framework's way of sorting
structural laws by the determining-state content the formed layer
permits.  Here a determining state is the internal state through
which later observable behavior is required to factor.  In some
substrates that state may expose witness-like data: for example,
explicit configurations, certificates, or geometric carriers.  In a
\emph{trace-state-only} column, by contrast, the admissible
determining-state information is restricted to trace observables on
a typed positive cone.  The readout is allowed to see aggregate
quantities such as \(\trace A\), but not a full state vector, a
hidden witness, or a richer invariant that would determine the
answer directly.

SDTC is the V-Differential trace-state-only law for involutive
self-dual closures.  Its visible object is not a hidden witness for
membership in \(\Fix(J)\); it is the anti-invariant ledger \(\AX\)
and its trace budget.  The readout \(\psim\) detects displacement
from the fixed locus, but the formed layer's determining information
is the positive-cone trace state: \(\trace \AX\), domination
inequalities, and vanishing bounds.  This is why the trace identity
and monotonicity of \(\trace\) are central.  The proof never needs
access to a denser internal state once the trace-state domination
records have been supplied.

Within this framing, SDTC joins parallel internal Six Birds
developments that organize other formed closures along the same
shape: a recognition source names the formed-layer structural
content, and an apparatus theorem extracts the advertised
mathematical consequence once that content is present.  Those
developments are mentioned here only to position SDTC inside the
program; none is an input to any definition, estimate, or theorem
below.

This section is organizational rather than deductive.  The
mathematical work begins in the next section: defining \(\IOL\),
constructing \(\AX\), stating the separation and domination
hypotheses, and proving the master theorem.
