\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

The framework context for SDTC is the Six Birds organization of
formed-layer structural laws.  In this paper, Six Birds Theory is
used as a typed and audited calculus for the closure-formation
discipline originating in Foundations I: it gives
names to the carriers, observables, comparison maps, and audit data
that a mathematical substrate presents when it is treated as a
formed object.  A \emph{formed closure} is such a typed bundle
together with the assumption that the bundle has been closed under
the relevant formation discipline.  In ordinary mathematical terms,
one may think of a substrate---algebraic, analytic, computational,
or geometric---equipped with a finite amount of visible structure
and with a declaration that this visible structure is stable under
the closure rules being used.  The admissibility schemas and typed
record interfaces used for this purpose are those of Foundations II
\cite{TsiokosFoundationsII2026}; the audited vocabulary of
visibility, threshold, gate, and claim-status data is part of the
Foundations III layer \cite{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026}.

This language does not replace the underlying mathematics; it
records which data are visible at a given layer and which comparison
principles are licensed there.  When the paper later writes an
involutive ledger \(\IOL\), a positive cone containing \(\AX\), or a
domination sequence \(B_n\), these objects do not add analytic
content.  They make explicit which carrier, readout, order relation,
trace functional, and audit conditions are in play.  The local
mathematics is the operator-style squeeze of the introduction; the
typed framework fixes the bookkeeping that keeps the squeeze inside
the same formed layer throughout.

Closure content is treated as a structural fact of the formed layer
rather than something to be re-derived from earlier framework
primitives.  Concretely, if a witness, comparison map, domination
sequence, or recognition record is part of what makes the layer
formed, the structural law is allowed to name that item as supplied
data.  This is not an axiom inserted into the surrounding
metatheory; it identifies which hypothesis about the substrate is
load-bearing.  For SDTC the load-bearing content is the availability
of domination records whose traces tend to zero, and the membrane
theorem proves what follows from those records.

This distinguishes a structural law from an apparatus theorem.  The
structural law names the formed-layer recognition content; in the
present paper \(\GamSDTC\) says that an admissibly formed involutive
self-dual closure supplies the trace-vanishing domination data
needed by the confinement mechanism.  The apparatus theorem is the
ordinary mathematical implication proved once those data are
present: a separating anti-invariant readout and bounds
\(\AX \preceq B_n\) with \(\trace B_n\to0\) force confinement on
\(\Fix(J)\).

The V-Differential classification is the framework's way of sorting
structural laws by what determining-state content the formed layer
permits.  Here a determining state means the internal state through
which later observable behavior is required to factor.  In some
substrates that state may expose witness-like data: for example,
explicit configurations, certificates, or geometric carriers.  In a
\emph{trace-state-only} column, by contrast, the admissible
determining-state information is restricted to trace observables on
a typed positive cone.  In standard terms, the readout is allowed to
see aggregate quantities such as \(\trace A\), but not a full state
vector, a hidden witness, or a richer invariant that would determine
the answer directly.

SDTC is the V-Differential trace-state-only law for involutive
self-dual closures.  Its visible object is not a hidden witness for
membership in \(\Fix(J)\); it is the anti-invariant ledger \(\AX\)
and its trace budget.  The readout \(\psim\) detects displacement
from the fixed locus, but the formed layer's determining information
is the positive-cone trace state: \(\trace \AX\), domination
inequalities, and vanishing bounds.  This is why the trace identity
and monotonicity of \(\trace\) are central.  The proof never needs
access to a denser internal state once the trace-state domination
records have been supplied.

Within this framing, SDTC joins two sibling structural laws in the
Six Birds program.  The no-needles structural law addresses
incompressible Navier--Stokes regularity by excluding a certain
formed-layer concentration pattern.  The CSL-SAT-hiddenness
structural law addresses the PvNP closure by treating satisfiability
information as hidden from the trace-state-only observation column.
Both have the same broad shape used here: a recognition source names
the formed-layer structural content, and an apparatus theorem
extracts the advertised mathematical consequence once that content
is present.  They are stated here only to position SDTC inside the
program.  Neither sibling law is an input to any definition,
estimate, or theorem in this paper.

This section is organizational rather than deductive.  The
mathematical work begins in the next section: defining \(\IOL\),
constructing \(\AX\), stating the separation and domination
hypotheses, and proving the master theorem.  The vocabulary
established here fixes how to read that work.  A formed closure is
the typed substrate; closure content is structural data supplied by
that substrate; trace-state-only is the restriction on what the
formed layer may reveal; and \(\GamSDTC\) is the named recognition
source for the involutive self-dual case.
