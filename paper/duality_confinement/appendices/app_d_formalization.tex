\section{Lean formalization}
\label{app:formalization}

This appendix records how the Lean development is cited in the
paper.  Body prose uses paper-level mathematical names and
distinguishes a theorem proved in Lean, a definition realized as a
Lean structure, a statement tracked through an explicit hypothesis,
and material with no Lean coverage.  Lean declarations and
module-level bookkeeping appear here.

\subsection*{Typed positive-cone encoding}

The formalization uses a mathlib-free typed positive cone as the
common substrate for \(\AX\), the bounds \(B_n\), the carrier
currency \(\Kminus\), and the tail terms \(T_n\).  The cone records
exactly the primitives used by the arguments in the paper: an order
\(\preceq\), a trace functional \(\trace\), trace monotonicity
under the order, and the positivity-to-zero principle that a
positive element with zero trace is the zero element.  The trace
identity for the anti-invariant ledger is encoded at this level:
the Gram-type construction of \(\AX\) is bundled with the assertion
that \(\trace\AX\) is the integral of \(\|\psim(x)\|^2\).

This abstraction is narrower than a full Hilbert-space operator
theory.  It is the formal substrate for the membrane argument
because the proof of the master theorem uses only order, trace
monotonicity, scalar squeezing, and positivity-to-zero; it does not
formalize trace-class operators, Hilbert-Schmidt maps, Schatten
ideals, or trace-norm topology
\citep{Simon2005,ReedSimon1980}.  Realizing the same theorem inside
the full positive trace-class cone remains an operator-theoretic
extension of the present work, not a hidden claim of the Lean
development.

\subsection*{Response-space typed carrier}

The body definition
(\Cref{def:duality_confinement:involutive-object-ledger}) takes the
response space \(Y\) to be a real Hilbert space, with \(\Jiso\) a
linear isometric involution on \(Y\).  In the Lean formalization,
\(Y\) is realized as a typed carrier with the primitives used by
the arguments above: an addition \(\mathtt{add}\), a scalar action
\(\mathtt{smul}\) over a typed \(\mathtt{Scalar}\) type, an inner
product \(\mathtt{inner}\), a norm \(\mathtt{norm}\), the
involution \(\mathtt{J\_iso}\), and the typed laws
\(\mathtt{J\_iso\_involutive}\), \(\mathtt{J\_iso\_linear}\),
\(\mathtt{J\_iso\_isometry}\), together with a readout
\(\mathtt{psi}\) and its equivariance field
\(\mathtt{psi\_equivariant}\).  This suffices for the proof steps
in the paper, which use these primitives only.  The full axiomatic
stack of a real Hilbert space---completeness, polarization
identity, parallelogram law, full bilinear/inner-product axioms,
\(\mathbb{R}\)-vector-space axioms beyond the bundled addition and
scalar action \citep{ReedSimon1980,Conway1990}---is not encoded in
Lean.  Realizing \(Y\) as a fully axiomatized real Hilbert space
inside Lean is an operator-theoretic extension of the present
work, not a hidden claim of the typed carrier.

\subsection*{Representation notes}

\paragraph{Douglas factorization.}
The classical mathematical content here is Douglas factorization
\citep{Douglas1966}: for bounded positive operators \(A=V^*V\) and
\(K=W^*W\) on a Hilbert space, domination \(A\preceq K\) is
equivalent to the existence of a contraction \(T\) with \(V=TW\).
The domination section uses this as operator-theoretic motivation.

In the mathlib-free typed-cone Lean encoding the factorization
theorem is represented by a typed \texttt{DouglasData} carrier.
The carrier records the operator-square-root presentation
\(A=V^*V\), \(K=W^*W\), the domination relation \(A\preceq K\),
the equivalent contractive factor witness \(T\) satisfying
\(V=TW\), and, as fields, the two directions
\texttt{order\_to\_factor} and \texttt{factor\_to\_order} of the
Douglas equivalence.  Downstream theorems read the implication
between domination and factorization from these typed fields.

The corresponding disclosure is that Lean packages the Douglas
equivalence at the typed-cone level by storing both directions as
typed fields of \texttt{DouglasData}, and
\texttt{douglasDomination} repackages those fields into the
\(\mathtt{Nonempty\ Domination}\leftrightarrow\mathtt{Nonempty\ ContractiveFactor}\)
form used by the bridge apparatus.  This is a typed-carrier
packaging rather than a Lean development of the full Hilbert-space
operator theorem, and not a Lean derivation of the equivalence
from operator-square-root primitives.

\paragraph{AM--GM in the scalar trace budget.}
The optimized scalar trace budget depends on the elementary
optimization
\[
  \inf_{t>0}\bigl((1+t)a+(1+t^{-1})b\bigr)
    = \bigl(\sqrt a+\sqrt b\bigr)^2 .
\]
In ordinary real arithmetic this follows from the AM--GM inequality
\citep{HardyLittlewoodPolya1952}, or equivalently from
non-negativity of \((\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2\).  The abstract scalar
encoding used here is weaker: it provides the scalar interface
needed by the trace-budget statement but does not supply enough
ordered real arithmetic to derive AM--GM substantively.

Accordingly, the Lean-side statement uses an explicit
\texttt{am\_gm\_optimization} hypothesis.  The optimized scalar trace
budget is tracked by the formalization harness as a typed projection
over that hypothesis; it is not advertised as a substantive Lean
derivation of AM--GM.  A future strengthening would add sufficient
typed real-arithmetic structure to express square roots,
non-negativity of squares, and the derivation of
\((\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2\geq0\).

\paragraph{Anti-invariant readout as typed field.}
The body definition of the anti-invariant readout is
\(\psim(x)=\Pminus\psi(x)\), and equivariance of \(\psi\) under the
involution forces \(\psim(Jx)=-\psim(x)\).  In the mathlib-free
typed-cone encoding the response-side projector \(\Pminus\) is not
represented, so \(\psim\) is realized in Lean as an independent
typed field of the separating-readout carrier and of the
anti-invariant-ledger carrier.  The two occurrences are linked by a
coherence hypothesis that they agree pointwise on the carrier, and
downstream theorems consume \(\psim\) only through these typed
fields.

The projection definition \(\psim=\Pminus\psi\) and the resulting
equivariance identity \(\psim(Jx)=-\psim(x)\) are therefore
paper-side mathematical content, not derived inside Lean from the
ledger's \(\psi\) and \(\Jiso\) fields.  A future strengthening
would add a response-side \(\Pminus\) primitive (or an anti-action
on the readout codomain) sufficient to derive the projection
identity and equivariance internally; in the present typed-cone
scope these are carried as typed-interface data.
