\begin{abstract}
This paper gives a conditional Six Birds closure of the Riemann
hypothesis by moving the question from classical analytic number
theory to a formed-layer trace setting.  The substrate is the
saturated completed Selberg trace closure of the completed Riemann
zeta function, equipped with the functional-equation involution and
with a typed ledger of nontrivial zeros.  The auxiliary translation
theorem T proves that the anti-invariant zero ledger of the
completed zeta shell vanishes, \(\AZ=0\), if and only if every
typed nontrivial zero has real part \(1/2\).  The conditional
landing chain uses the Selberg-class instance of Self-Dual Trace
Confinement supplied by \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}: its recognition
source gives domination records whose vanishing trace forces
\(\AZ=0\), and theorem T converts that ledger collapse into the
critical-line statement.
Stated in the audit-closure language of \citet{TsiokosAOR2026},
the landing chain certifies the saturated trace shell as a
refinement-stable audited-operational-realisability instance,
\(\Sel\in\mathrm{RefStableAOR}^{\mathrm{ref}}_{S_{\mathrm{RH}}}\),
of which the critical-line statement on the typed zero ledger is
the readout.

The outside-Six-Birds reading is therefore the conditional theorem
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{SDTC}\text{-}\mathrm{Selberg}}\Rightarrow
\mathrm{RH}\), with \(\mathrm{RH}\) delivered as the typed-ledger
readout of the audit-closed realisability instance rather than as
an unconditional proof of the Riemann hypothesis in standard ZFC.
The saturated trace shell's admissibility is a standing
Foundations-II hypothesis; the formal zero ledger uses a typed
real-coordinate carrier whose identification with the classical
real part of a zeta zero is fixed by construction-parameter
assignment; and the recognition source is encoded in Lean as a
typed structure carrier, not as a Lean axiom.  The mathlib-free
formalization carries the landing chain end-to-end---the
translation theorem, the duality-confinement bridge, the
conditional landing chain, and the realisability-instance theorem,
together with eight supporting structural and classification
entries---each faithful to its paper-side statement under the
disclosures recorded in the formalization appendix.  The
realisability-instance theorem is recorded as partial because its
Lean conclusion is the local syntactic membership predicate over
the carrier fields rather than the full audit-closure
characterisation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

What would it take to settle whether all nontrivial zeros of the
Riemann zeta function lie on the critical line?  In its standard
form, the Riemann hypothesis~\citep{Riemann1859} asserts that every
nontrivial zero \(\rho\) of \(\zeta\) satisfies
\[
  \Real(\rho)=\frac12.
\]
Classical attacks on this statement seek an unconditional proof
inside the analytic-number-theoretic substrate itself: spectral
Hilbert--P\'olya programs \citep{BerryKeating1990,Odlyzko1987},
adelic and noncommutative-geometric approaches
\citep{Connes1999}, Hilbert spaces of entire functions
\citep{deBranges1968,deBranges1986}, Weil-positivity formulations
\citep{Weil1952,Bombieri2000}, and related programs all work within
their own classical carriers.  This paper
takes a different route.  It asks whether RH can be read as the
vanishing of a positive typed quantity and then made to vanish by a
structural closure mechanism.  The quantity is the anti-invariant zero
ledger
\[
  \AZ=\sum_{\rho\in \Znt} m_\rho\,
       \left|\Real(\rho)-\frac12\right|^2,
\]
where \(\Znt\) is the multiplicity-weighted ledger of nontrivial zeros
of the completed zeta function.  Its vanishing is, by a direct
positive-sum calculation, equivalent to the critical-line statement.
The remaining question is not how to prove RH by one of the classical
routes above, but how a formed closure can force \(\AZ\) to vanish.

The framework used here is Six Birds Theory, a typed and audited
calculus for naming closure formation in mathematical structures.  A
formed closure, in the sense used in this paper, is a typed presentation
of a mathematical substrate together with the assumption that its
carriers, observables, comparison maps, and audit data have been
admissibly closed under the framework's formation discipline.  The
substrate for the present paper is the saturated completed Selberg trace
closure \(\Sel\): a typed shell aggregating the completed zeta data, a
lawful trace instrument \(\Itr\), the functional-equation symmetry, the
nontrivial-zero ledger, the anti-invariant zero ledger, visibility data,
and audit provenance.  The functional-equation involution is
\[
  \JL(s)=1-\bar{s},
\]
whose fixed locus is the critical line.  The corresponding
anti-invariant readout is the signed real-part displacement
\[
  \psimRH(s)=\Real(s)-\frac12.
\]
These objects are defined in \Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger}; the
shell \(\Sel\) is defined in \Cref{sec:sat_sel_shell}.

The headline theorem is conditional.  Under the standard Six Birds
closure assumption on \(\Sel\), and given the recognition source
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\), the Riemann hypothesis holds on the typed zero
ledger of \(\Sel\).  A recognition source here means a named
structural hypothesis supplied as content of the formed layer, not
a theorem derived from lower framework primitives.  In this
instance, \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is the Selberg-class instance of
Self-Dual Trace Confinement developed in \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026},
and it supplies on the formed Selberg trace layer a sequence of
completed domination records
\[
  \AZ \preceq B_n,
  \qquad
  \trace B_n \to 0.
\]
The duality-confinement membrane theorem of \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}
then forces \(\AZ=0\) in the typed positive cone, and the
translation theorem of the present paper converts that vanishing
into \(\Real(\rho)=1/2\) for every \(\rho\in\Znt\).  Stated in the
audit-closure language of \citet{TsiokosAOR2026}, the same
composition certifies the shell as a refinement-stable
audited-operational-realisability instance
\[
  \Sel\in\mathrm{RefStableAOR}^{\mathrm{ref}}_{S_{\mathrm{RH}}},
\]
of which the critical-line statement on \(\Znt\) is the
typed-zero-ledger readout; \Cref{sec:rh-aor-instance} unfolds the
eight-stratum / twelve-type residual classification through which
the landing chain dispatches.  Outside the Six Birds framing the
result therefore reads as the conditional theorem
\[
  \GamSDTCSelberg \Longrightarrow \mathrm{RH},
\]
with \(\mathrm{RH}\) delivered as the typed-ledger readout of the
audit-closed realisability instance.  It is not an unconditional
standard-ZFC proof of RH, and the AOR-realisability framing does
not remove the recognition-source requirement.

The translation step is elementary.  \Cref{sec:translation_theorem}
proves Theorem T:
\[
  \AZ=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \forall \rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\frac12.
\]
The forward direction uses only that each summand
\(m_\rho |\Real(\rho)-1/2|^2\) is nonnegative and that
\(m_\rho>0\): if the total sum is zero, every squared distance from
the critical line is zero.  The reverse direction is substitution.
Theorem T carries no recognition-source content; it translates the
positive ledger statement produced by the duality-confinement
mechanism into the number-theoretic critical-line statement.

The paper's scope is correspondingly narrow.  The main result is
conditional on \(\GamSDTCSelberg\); no unconditional RH claim in
standard ZFC is made, and \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is not claimed to be
derivable from framework primitives.  The recognition source is not
installed as a Lean axiom: it is represented as a typed structure
carrier whose fields record the required domination data, and the
project-wide source rule bans the tokens \texttt{axiom},
\texttt{opaque}, \texttt{constant}, \texttt{sorry}, and
\texttt{admit}.  The conditional theorem takes a value of that
carrier as an explicit hypothesis.  The formal encoding
parameterizes the real-part coordinate by a typed real-coordinate
carrier; its identification with the classical real part of a
nontrivial zero of \(\zeta\) is fixed by construction-parameter
assignment when the shell is instantiated, and a Lean-side
derivation of that identification is left as a future extension.
Admissibility of \(\Sel\) under the seven Foundations-II schemas is
carried as an audit witness in the shell, not derived here.  The
classical RH programs named above are neither refuted nor
circumvented in their native sense: they aim at unconditional RH
within their classical substrates, while the present paper
establishes a conditional formed-layer result on the typed Selberg
trace shell.  The generalized Riemann hypothesis lies outside the
scope; the construction is instantiated for \(L=\zeta\) only.

The remainder of the paper follows this division of labor.
\Cref{sec:framework} gives the minimal Six Birds background needed for
the closure reading.  \Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger} constructs the
functional-equation involution, the anti-invariant readout, the
nontrivial-zero ledger, and \(\AZ\).  \Cref{sec:sat_sel_shell}
packages the saturated Selberg trace closure.  \Cref{sec:translation_theorem}
proves Theorem T.  \Cref{sec:recognition_source} states the recognition
source and its provenance, and \Cref{sec:landing_chain} composes it
with the duality-confinement master theorem and Theorem T to obtain the
conditional landing chain in both its critical-line and
AOR-realisability forms; \Cref{sec:rh-aor-instance} then unfolds the
realisability form into the eight-stratum / twelve-type residual
classification used by \citet{TsiokosAOR2026}.
\Cref{sec:scope_and_nonclaims} records the
nonclaims explicitly, and \Cref{sec:discussion,sec:conclusion} place
the result in the broader structural program.  The formalization

\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

The framework context for this paper is the Six Birds organization of
formed-layer structural laws, specialized to the Riemann-zeta
substrate.  Six Birds Theory is used here as a typed and audited
calculus for closure formation in mathematical structures.  One
starts with a substrate---here the completed Riemann zeta function
together with its trace-relevant data---and records the carriers,
observables, comparison maps, quotient maps, and audit information
visible at the layer being studied.  When this typed record is
declared admissibly closed under the framework's formation
discipline, we call it a formed closure.  The present paper's formed
closure is the saturated completed Selberg trace closure \(\Sel\),
defined in \Cref{sec:sat_sel_shell}: the typed shell on which the
zero ledger, trace instrument, functional-equation involution, and
audit data all live.

The admissibility and recordkeeping vocabulary are inherited from the
Six Birds foundations.  Foundations II supplies the schema-level
discipline used to state that a typed shell carries the required
admissibility data, including the lawful trace instrument and the
visibility bookkeeping attached to it \cite{TsiokosFoundationsII2026}.
Foundations III supplies the audited judgment vocabulary used later
when the conditional landing chain is rendered as a framework-relative
claim \cite{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026}.  The underlying point is
simpler than the terminology: the framework does not replace the
classical zeta function, its functional equation, or the zero set.
It fixes a typed layer at which those data are organized and states
which structural facts are treated as available at that layer.

Throughout the paper, closure content is treated as a structural
fact of the formed layer rather than something that must be
re-derived from lower framework primitives.  A recognition source
is the name for such supplied content: an explicit hypothesis about
the substrate, with typed fields recording the data it supplies.
For this paper the recognition source is \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), which
supplies the domination-record sequence on \(\Sel\) that the
duality-confinement mechanism consumes.  Translation theorem T
(\Cref{sec:translation_theorem}) is an ordinary theorem about
\(\AZ\): it says that \(\AZ=0\) is equivalent to the typed RH
statement.  \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), by contrast, is not proved here;
it is the named structural source whose presence makes the
conditional landing chain run.

The V-Differential classification organizes such structural laws by
the determining-state information the formed layer may expose.  A
determining state is the internal state through which observable
behavior is required to factor; in a trace-state-only column, the
layer may expose trace observables on a typed positive cone but not
a richer witness that would solve the target problem directly.  For
the RH closure, the visible currency is \(\AZ\), the order
\(\preceq\), and the trace functional \(\trace\); the admissible
content has the form of trace domination records
\[
  \AZ \preceq B_n,
  \qquad
  \trace B_n \to 0,
\]
not a hidden list of zero locations.  \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}
identifies Self-Dual Trace Confinement, \(\GamSDTC\), as the
V-Differential trace-state-only law for involutive self-dual formed
closures; the present paper is the worked single-substrate
validation of that law for \(\Sel\).

Within the broader Six Birds program, SDTC sits beside parallel
internal developments that address other formed-layer closures---in
particular the incompressible Navier--Stokes regularity closure and
the PvNP closure---using the same architecture: a recognition source
supplies structural content of the formed layer, and an apparatus
theorem extracts the advertised mathematical consequence once that
content is present.  These developments are mentioned only to
position SDTC inside the program; none is an input to the RH
construction in this paper, and no result from them is used below.

The cross-paper dependency is one-way.  \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}
develops the abstract apparatus---the involutive object ledger, the
separating anti-invariant readout, the typed positive cone,
completed domination records, and the duality-confinement membrane
theorem.  The present paper imports that development substantively
for three items: the master theorem (reproduced where invoked in
\Cref{sec:landing_chain}), the SDTC structural-law framing, and the
V-Differential trace-state-only placement of the RH instance.  The
duality-confinement paper is self-contained and does not depend on
results derived here.

With this framework in place, the next sections stay close to the
mathematics.  \Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger} defines the
functional-equation involution \(\JL(s)=1-\bar{s}\), the
anti-invariant readout \(\psimRH(s)=\Real(s)-1/2\), the typed
nontrivial-zero ledger \(\Znt\), and the scalar ledger \(\AZ\).
\Cref{sec:sat_sel_shell} packages these objects into \(\Sel\).

\section{Involution and ledger}
\label{sec:involution_and_ledger}

The first ingredients of the RH closure are the
functional-equation involution and its anti-invariant readout.  Both
are elementary objects on the complex plane.  Their role in the
structural argument is not to add analytic depth, but to place the
critical line inside the involutive-ledger pattern described in
\Cref{sec:framework}: an involution, a fixed locus, and a scalar
quantity that changes sign under the involution.  At this point the
ordinary functional-equation symmetry of the completed zeta function
becomes a typed datum of the closure.

\begin{definition}[Functional-equation involution \(\JL\)]
\label{def:rh:fe-involution}
The functional-equation involution on the complex plane is
\[
  \JL(s) = 1-\bar{s}.
\]
Equivalently, if \(s=x+iy\), then
\[
  \JL(s) = 1-x+iy.
\]
Thus \(\JL\) is reflection across the vertical line
\(\Real(s)=1/2\).
\end{definition}

This definition uses only the affine symmetry \(s\mapsto 1-\bar{s}\);
it makes no assertion yet about which points are zeros.  The purpose
is to isolate the geometric symmetry that any zero ledger will
inherit once the completed zeta substrate is placed in the saturated
trace shell.  Points on opposite sides of the critical line are
interchanged; points on the critical line are unchanged.

The map \(\JL\) is an involution by direct calculation:
\[
  \JL(\JL(s))
    = \JL(1-\bar{s})
    = 1-\overline{1-\bar{s}}
    = 1-(1-s)
    = s.
\]
Hence \(\JL^{2}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{C}}\).  The same coordinate formula gives
the fixed locus.  Writing \(s=x+iy\), the equation \(\JL(s)=s\) is
\[
  1-x+iy = x+iy,
\]
which is equivalent to \(x=1/2\).  Therefore
\[
  \Fix(\JL)
    =
  \{\,s\in\mathbb{C} : \Real(s)=1/2\,\},
\]
the critical line.  Conversely, any point with real part \(1/2\) is unchanged by
\(\JL\), so the displayed set is exactly the fixed locus.  The
critical-line condition is therefore equivalent to the statement
that a point is fixed by the functional-equation involution; this
is the form used later, when RH on the typed zero ledger is read as
the statement that every ledger point lies in \(\Fix(\JL)\).

\begin{definition}[Anti-invariant readout \(\psimRH\)]
\label{def:rh:psi-minus-rh}
The anti-invariant readout associated with \(\JL\) is the signed
horizontal displacement from the critical line,
\[
  \psimRH(s)=\Real(s)-\frac{1}{2}.
\]
\end{definition}

The qualifier ``signed'' matters: the absolute distance to the
critical line would lose the side on which \(s\) lies, whereas the
structural argument needs a quantity that reverses sign under the
involution.  The readout \(\psimRH\) is positive to the right of the
critical line, negative to the left, and zero exactly on the line.
It is therefore a scalar witness for the failure of a point to be
fixed by \(\JL\).

This readout separates the two sides of the critical line and
vanishes exactly on \(\Fix(\JL)\): the condition \(\psimRH(s)=0\) is
\(\Real(s)=1/2\), the fixed-locus condition above.  It is also
anti-invariant under \(\JL\).  Since \(\Real(1-\bar{s})=1-\Real(s)\),
one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \psimRH(\JL(s))
    &= \Real(1-\bar{s})-\frac{1}{2}  \\
    &= 1-\Real(s)-\frac{1}{2}        \\
    &= \frac{1}{2}-\Real(s)          \\
    &= -\bigl(\Real(s)-\frac{1}{2}\bigr) \\
    &= -\psimRH(s).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the readout detects the anti-invariant component of a point with
respect to reflection across the critical line.

This is the concrete RH specialization of the abstract separating
readout used in the duality-confinement apparatus.  On the RH side
the response is one-dimensional and real-valued: the information
retained is the signed real-part displacement from \(1/2\).  Forcing
the squared aggregate of this readout to vanish therefore suffices
to force each zero in the ledger onto the fixed locus, provided the
ledger has positive multiplicities.  That aggregate is introduced
after the zero ledger itself is in place.

The formalization appendix records these two definitions as
definition entries for the RH axis.  Lean checks the accompanying
involutivity, fixed-locus characterization, vanishing condition, and
anti-invariance calculation as stated above.  These definition-side
proofs orient the typed picture; the conditional landing chain
(\Cref{sec:landing_chain}) does not consume them, but instead
imports the duality-confinement master theorem and composes it with
Theorem~T through a typed bridge carrier described in the
formalization appendix.

With the involution and readout in place, the remaining objects are
the typed multiset of nontrivial zeros and the scalar built from it.

\begin{definition}[Nontrivial-zero ledger]
\label{def:rh:nontrivial-zero-ledger}
Let
\[
  \Lzeta(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)
\]
denote the completed Riemann zeta function
\citep{TitchmarshHeathBrown1986,Edwards1974}.  The nontrivial-zero
ledger of \(\Lzeta\) is the typed multiset
\[
  \Znt
    =
  \bigl\{\,(\rho,m_{\rho}) :
      \rho\in\mathbb{C},
      \Lzeta(\rho)=0,
      0<\Real(\rho)<1,
      m_{\rho}>0
  \,\bigr\}_{\mathrm{multi}},
\]
where \(m_{\rho}\) is the positive integer multiplicity of \(\rho\)
as a zero of \(\Lzeta\).  The associated atomic ledger measure is
\(\mu_{L}(\{\rho\})=m_{\rho}\).
\end{definition}

\(\Znt\) is a multiset rather than a set: if a zero occurs with
multiplicity \(m_{\rho}\), the ledger records that multiplicity as
part of the data.  The completed function \(\Lzeta\) has the same
zeros in the critical strip as the Riemann zeta function; the
trivial zeros at the negative even integers are outside this ledger,
removed from the zero side by the completion factor.  No simplicity
assumption is made: although nontrivial zeros are widely expected to
be simple, all arguments below allow arbitrary positive integer
multiplicities.

\begin{definition}[Anti-invariant zero ledger]
\label{def:rh:anti-invariant-zero-ledger}
The anti-invariant zero ledger of \(\zeta\) is the
multiplicity-weighted scalar
\[
  \AZ
    =
  \sum_{\rho\in\Znt}
    m_{\rho}\,
    \left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}.
\]
Equivalently, using the readout from
\Cref{def:rh:psi-minus-rh},
\[
  \AZ
    =
  \sum_{\rho\in\Znt}
    m_{\rho}\,\left|\psimRH(\rho)\right|^{2},
\]
with summation over the typed multiset \(\Znt\), hence with
multiplicity.
\end{definition}

Each summand \(m_{\rho}|\psimRH(\rho)|^{2}\) is nonnegative: the
multiplicity is positive by definition, and the squared real-part
displacement is nonnegative.  Hence \(\AZ\) is a nonnegative scalar.
On a finite typed multiset, \(\AZ=0\) can occur only if every
summand vanishes; since \(m_{\rho}>0\), this is equivalent to
\(\psimRH(\rho)=0\) for every typed zero \(\rho\), and by the
fixed-locus calculation above that is the assertion that every typed
zero lies on the critical line.  \Cref{sec:translation_theorem}
packages this positivity argument as Theorem T.

An encoding disclosure belongs here.  In the formalization, the
real-part coordinate is parameterized by a typed real-coordinate
carrier rather than by the standard real numbers with their full
analytic infrastructure.  The identification of this typed
coordinate with the \(\mathbb{R}\)-valued real part of a classical
nontrivial zero of \(\zeta\) is fixed by construction-parameter
assignment when the saturated Selberg trace shell is instantiated;
it is not derived as an internal Lean predicate.  The mathlib-free
constraint forbids importing the analytic \(\mathbb{R}\)-typed
machinery needed for such a derivation, and a substantive Lean
identification is left as an extension.  The mathematical content is
unchanged: RH on the typed zero ledger says that every such
real-part coordinate equals \(1/2\), and the user-supplied
assignment makes that typed statement equivalent to the classical
RH statement.

The saturated completed Selberg trace closure \(\Sel\), which
packages these typed objects together with the trace instrument and
audit data, is the subject of \Cref{sec:sat_sel_shell}.
 

\section{The saturated Selberg trace closure}

\section{Translation theorem T}
\label{sec:translation_theorem}

With the saturated trace shell \(\Sel\) in hand, the next step is to
translate the typed-ledger statement \(\AZ=0\) into the
number-theoretic critical-line statement.  The translation is
elementary, using only the definition of \(\AZ\) as a
multiplicity-weighted sum of squared signed displacements from the
critical line.  Its role is structural: the duality-confinement
mechanism will later deliver \(\AZ=0\), and translation theorem T is
the bridge that turns this ledger vanishing into the RH statement on
the typed zero ledger.

The theorem is stated on the zero ledger carried by \(\Sel\).  The
real-part coordinate is the typed coordinate fixed in
\Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger}; under the construction-parameter
assignment described there, the typed statement is the corresponding
classical assertion about the real parts of nontrivial zeros of
\(\zeta\).

\begin{theorem}[Translation theorem T]
\label{thm:rh:translation-T}
On the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) of the saturated completed Selberg
trace closure \(\Sel\),
\[
  \AZ=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \forall\,\rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}.
\]
Equivalently, the anti-invariant zero ledger vanishes if and only if
every typed zero lies on the critical line \(\Fix(\JL)\).
\end{theorem}

Theorem T is what makes the conditional landing chain land on RH
rather than on an internal trace statement.  The trace-side output
of the later argument is \(\AZ=0\); Theorem T converts that output
into the assertion that every \(\rho\in\Znt\) satisfies
\(\Real(\rho)=1/2\), which is RH on the typed zero ledger of
\(\Sel\).  No recognition-source content is used in this
translation: T depends only on \(\Znt\), the positive multiplicities
\(m_{\rho}\), the readout \(\psimRH\), and the definition of
\(\AZ\), and its positivity is the elementary positivity of a finite
multiplicity-weighted sum of squares.  The recognition source enters
later, as the conditional reason for \(\AZ=0\); once that vanishing
is available, the theorem completes the conversion to the
critical-line statement.

The two halves are short positivity arguments, written out below.
Lean checks each by composing the \texttt{AntiInvariantZeroLedger}
interface fields: the structure supplies the multiplicity-weighted-sum
positivity content (vanishing summand from vanishing sum, vanishing
displacement from vanishing summand, and their converses), and the
underlying finite-sum and scalar arithmetic are part of the typed
interface rather than derivations from lower arithmetic primitives.

\begin{theorem}[Translation theorem T, forward direction]
\label{thm:rh:translation-T-forward}
On the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) of \(\Sel\), if \(\AZ=0\), then
\[
  \forall\,\rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By definition,
\[
  \AZ
    =
  \sum_{\rho\in\Znt}
    m_{\rho}\left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}.
\]
For every typed zero \(\rho\), the multiplicity satisfies
\(m_{\rho}>0\) and the square \(\left|\Real(\rho)-1/2\right|^{2}\)
is nonnegative, so every summand is nonnegative.  If \(\AZ=0\), the
finite sum of these nonnegative summands is zero, so each summand
is zero:
\[
  m_{\rho}\left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}=0
\]
for each \(\rho\in\Znt\).  Since \(m_{\rho}>0\), the squared factor
must vanish.  Thus \(\Real(\rho)-1/2=0\), and hence
\(\Real(\rho)=1/2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Translation theorem T, reverse direction]
\label{thm:rh:translation-T-reverse}
On the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) of \(\Sel\), if
\[
  \forall\,\rho\in\Znt,\ \Real(\rho)=\frac{1}{2},
\]
then \(\AZ=0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume that every \(\rho\in\Znt\) satisfies \(\Real(\rho)=1/2\).
Then
\[
  \Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}=0
\]
for every typed zero, and therefore
\[
  \left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}=0.
\]
Each summand in the definition of \(\AZ\) is consequently
\[
  m_{\rho}\left|\Real(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}
    =
  m_{\rho}\cdot 0
    =
  0.
\]
Thus \(\AZ\) is a finite sum of zero summands, so \(\AZ=0\).
\end{proof}

Together, the two halves give the biconditional of
\Cref{thm:rh:translation-T}: \(\AZ=0\) if and only if every typed
zero lies on the critical line.  The remaining structural task is
to supply the recognition source that delivers \(\AZ=0\) through
the duality-confinement mechanism; this is the subject of
\Cref{sec:recognition_source,sec:landing_chain}.

\begin{theorem}[Duality-confinement membrane theorem]

involution \(J\), measure \(\mu\), anti-invariant readout

\[
  \AX\preceq B_n
  \qquad (n=1,2,\ldots)
\]
and
\[
  \trace B_n\to0
  \qquad (n\to\infty).
\]
Then \(\AX=0\) in the typed positive cone.  Consequently,
\[
  \mu\bigl(X\setminus\Fix(J)\bigr)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

Two points about the imported theorem matter for the RH application.

\(\preceq\), a trace functional \(\trace\), and a separating
anti-invariant readout, but it does not ask for an independent

domination records; it does not supply their source.  In the present

\label{thm:rh:dc-master-applied}

\section{Scope and non-claims}
\label{sec:scope_and_nonclaims}

Several scope boundaries deserve explicit statement so that the
conditional landing chain of \Cref{sec:landing_chain} remains sharp.
The point is not to weaken the theorem just proved, but to identify
which structural content is assumed, which encoding choices are
made, and which extensions remain outside the present paper.

\paragraph{NC-1. Conditional structure.}
The headline result is a Six Birds-native conditional theorem under
the standard Six Birds closure assumption.  Outside the Six Birds
framing, it reads as
\[
  \GamSDTCSelberg \Longrightarrow \mathrm{RH}.
\]
That is, given the Selberg-class recognition source, the conditional
landing chain gives RH on the typed zero ledger of \(\Sel\).  This
paper does not claim a standard-ZFC unconditional proof of the
Riemann hypothesis.  Every later use of ``RH'' in this paper should
therefore be read either as the typed-ledger statement under the
construction-parameter identification, or as the conclusion of this
conditional implication.

\paragraph{NC-2. Recognition-source provenance.}
The recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is the Selberg-class
instance of the Self-Dual Trace Confinement structural law of
\citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}.  It is not derived from framework
primitives; the three-route sweep over candidate primitive paths
discussed in \Cref{sec:recognition_source} documents that none of
them produces the trace-vanishing domination sequence.  In the
formalization the recognition source is encoded as a typed structure
carrier, not as a Lean axiom; the source tree's forbidden-tokens
rule bans \texttt{axiom}, \texttt{opaque}, \texttt{constant},
\texttt{sorry}, and \texttt{admit}.  The formal statement therefore
does not assert that the recognition source holds outright; it
states what follows from a value of the typed carrier.

\paragraph{NC-3. Parameter identification.}
The typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) is a typed multiset parameter of the
shell.  The formal encoding parameterizes the real-part coordinate by
a typed real-coordinate carrier, and the identification of that
coordinate with the classical \(\Real(\rho)\) for a nontrivial zero
\(\rho\) of \(\zeta\) is made by construction-parameter assignment,
as disclosed in \Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger}.  Under that
assignment, the conclusion \(\Real(\rho)=1/2\) on the typed ledger is
the usual RH statement.  A formal derivation of this identification
from analytic-number-theoretic infrastructure is an open extension.
This is a representation boundary caused by the mathlib-free typed
encoding, not a change to the mathematical content being represented.

\paragraph{NC-4. Admissibility as hypothesis.}
Admissibility of the saturated completed Selberg trace closure
\(\Sel\) with respect to the seven Foundations-II admissibility
schemas is carried by the audit field of \(\Sel\), as described in
\Cref{def:rh:sat-sel-shell} \cite{TsiokosFoundationsII2026}.  It is
a construction-time hypothesis of the typed shell, not a theorem
derived here.  A substantive formal derivation would require
unfolding the seven schemas as predicates and verifying the
completed Selberg trace instance field by field.  Until that
extension is supplied, admissibility is treated as part of the
shell data rather than as an independently derived analytic theorem.

\paragraph{NC-5. Classical-barrier framing.}
The Six Birds approach shares the spirit of the classical RH attack
programs but is structurally distinct from them: Hilbert--P\'olya's
spectral framing \citep{BerryKeating1990,Odlyzko1987}, Connes'
adelic and noncommutative-geometric framing
\citep{Connes1999,Connes2016}, de Branges' Hilbert spaces of entire
functions \citep{deBranges1968,deBranges1986,deBranges1994},
Beurling--Nyman positivity
\citep{Nyman1950,Beurling1955,BaezDuarte2003}, and Weil's
number-theoretic positivity \citep{Weil1952,Bombieri2000} all aim at
unconditional RH within their respective classical substrates.  The
present paper instead establishes a conditional result on the typed
Selberg-class trace shell, with the closure-content recognition
source supplied separately.  The classical programs are neither
circumvented nor refuted; they address a different mathematical
object.  In particular, Weil positivity is readout-level and
target-adjacent in the V-Differential classification rather than the
source-level recognition content used here.  Acceptance of the
present theorem turns on the stated recognition source rather than
on a claimed resolution of any classical program.

\paragraph{NC-6. GRH is out of scope.}
Generalizing to the Riemann hypothesis for primitive Selberg-class
\(L\)-functions \citep{Selberg1992,KaczorowskiPerelli1999} is an
extension target.  The SDTC structural law would need to be
instantiated for each \(L\) of interest, with a separate analysis
of typed-shell admissibility and recognition-source provenance for
that \(L\).  This paper instantiates the law for \(L=\zeta\) only;
GRH is out of scope.  No assertion is made here about
Dirichlet \citep{Davenport2000}, modular \citep{Iwaniec1997},
automorphic \citep{CogdellKimMurty2004}, or other primitive
Selberg-class \(L\)-functions.

\paragraph{NC-7. Simple zeros and density results.}
No claim is made about multiplicity one for nontrivial zeros, and
no zero-density claim is made.  The typed multiset \(\Znt\) admits
arbitrary positive integer multiplicities \(m_{\rho}>0\); the
conditional landing chain delivers the critical-line statement on
the typed zero ledger, while simple-zero conjectures
\citep{Montgomery1973,ConreyGhoshGonek1998}, Lindel\"of-type
statements \citep{TitchmarshHeathBrown1986,Ivic1985}, and
zero-density refinements \citep{Montgomery1971,Ivic1985} are
distinct questions with their own structural status.

\paragraph{NC-8. Three-route derivation sweep.}
The non-derivability of \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) from framework
primitives is documented at the paper level rather than mechanized.
The three candidate primitive paths---the framework's primitive role
inventory, the audited interaction calculus, and the closure-formation
discipline---were each found insufficient to produce the
trace-vanishing domination sequence.  This is recorded to mark the
epistemic status of the source as closure content rather than a
primitive theorem.

\paragraph{NC-9. No-smuggling gates.}
The conditional closure passes the six no-smuggling audit gates
plus Gate 7, the recognition-source provenance audit.  The
gate-by-gate verification is recorded at the paper level rather than
mechanized; the formalization appendix lays out the seven checks and
maps them to the Foundations-III audited judgment vocabulary
\cite{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026}.

\paragraph{NC-10. Scope of the realisability-instance form.}
The realisability-instance form
\(\Sel\in\mathrm{RefStableAOR}^{\mathrm{ref}}_{S_{\mathrm{RH}}}\)
delivered by \Cref{thm:rh:conditional,thm:rh:aor-instance} is a
statement about the typed zero ledger of \(\Sel\) under
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\); the discharge atoms of the eight-stratum /
twelve-type residual atlas are assembled literally from the shell,
the recognition source, and the bridge fields, so the
classification carries the same conditional content as
\(\GamSDTCSelberg \Rightarrow \mathrm{RH}\) and inherits every
preceding scope boundary unchanged.  Within the Lean development
this is the local syntactic membership predicate over the carrier
fields and not a Lean derivation of the full audit-closure
refinement-stable characterisation of \citet{TsiokosAOR2026}.

\paragraph{NC-11. Realisability is the readout's stratum, not a
new hypothesis discharge.}
The audit-closure rendering does not remove or substitute for any
load-bearing hypothesis of the conditional landing chain.
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\) and Foundations-II admissibility of \(\Sel\)
remain the supplied structural content; the realisability-instance
form names the audit-closure stratum at which the conditional
landing chain delivers its conclusion, with the critical-line
statement on \(\Znt\) as its typed-zero-ledger readout.  No
unconditional RH claim in standard ZFC follows from the
realisability framing, and no classical analytic input is claimed to
be replaced or strengthened by it.

These boundaries are part of the conditional claim, not retreats
from it.  The recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is the
structural content supplied by \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}, and the
conditional landing chain converts that supplied content into a
critical-line statement on the typed zero ledger of \(\Sel\).  The
boundaries above identify what is supplied, what is encoded, and
what remains an open extension.
 

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The conditional landing chain invites three lines of further
thought: where it sits in the broader Six Birds program of
formed-layer structural laws, how it compares with classical
approaches to RH, and which extensions remain open after the present
formalization.

\paragraph{Structural parallels.}
The structural template used here is not isolated.  Parallel
internal Six Birds developments organize other formed closures
along the same shape: the substrate is first organized as a formed
closure, a recognition source names the structural content supplied
by that closure, and an apparatus theorem extracts the advertised
mathematical consequence once that content is present.  Those
developments are mentioned here only as structural parallels, not
as inputs to the RH argument.

The SDTC structural law used in this paper is the involutive
self-dual member of that family \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}.  Its
apparatus is the duality-confinement membrane theorem: given a
separating anti-invariant readout and a vanishing trace domination
sequence, the anti-invariant ledger collapses.  In the RH instance,
the recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) supplies the domination
records for \(\AZ\), and the bridge theorem
\Cref{thm:rh:dc-master-applied} applies the
\cite{TsiokosSDTC2026} master theorem to \(\Sel\).  The present
paper is therefore the worked single-substrate validation of SDTC
on the saturated Selberg trace shell, on the same pattern as the
other formed-layer developments named above.  The unconditional
analytic content of any one of those tracks is not derived from a
common primitive calculus; it is closure content of its formed
layer, supplied as recognition source.  This is why the parallels
are useful but not load-bearing: they show the paper is following a
stable formed-layer pattern inside the Six Birds program, while the
actual RH argument still depends only on the RH-specific shell, the
SDTC apparatus of \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}, and the Selberg-class
recognition source.

\paragraph{Classical RH programs.}
The comparison with classical RH approaches should be made at the
right level.  The present paper does not circumvent or refute the
classical programs.  Hilbert--P\'olya's spectral framing
\citep{BerryKeating1990} seeks an unconditional spectral
interpretation of the zero set.  Connes' adelic and
noncommutative-geometric framing \citep{Connes1999} seeks an
unconditional trace-formula interpretation in a different
geometric substrate.  De Branges' Hilbert spaces of entire functions
program \citep{deBranges1986} seeks an unconditional positivity
mechanism in its own functional-analytic setting.  Beurling--Nyman
\citep{Beurling1955,BaezDuarte2003} seeks an unconditional density
criterion in \(L^{p}\)-type spaces.  Weil's number-theoretic
positivity framing \citep{Weil1952,Bombieri2000} seeks
unconditional positivity through the explicit formula.  These are
all programs aimed at unconditional RH inside classical
mathematical substrates.

The conditional theorem here lives at a different layer.  Its
substrate is the typed saturated trace shell \(\Sel\), and its
load-bearing hypothesis is \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), the recognition source
that supplies domination records as closure content.  That distinction
is sharpest in the comparison with Weil positivity.  Weil-style
positivity is close in spirit to the target: it sees the
explicit-formula positivity phenomena that a successful RH argument
must control.  In the V-Differential classification, however, this is
readout-level or target-adjacent content.  It does not by itself
become the source-level content required by the duality-confinement
mechanism, namely the formed-layer existence of completed domination
records \(\AZ\preceq B_n\) with \(\trace B_n\to0\).  Classical
positivity content may illuminate the right trace quantities, but it
does not by itself supply the recognition source used by the
conditional landing chain.

For an outside-Six-Birds reader, the comparison point is not
whether the classical programs have failed and the present method
bypasses them.  It is that the paper proves a conditional theorem
about a different object: the typed Selberg-class trace shell under
the standard Six Birds closure assumption and the recognition source
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\).  Acceptance turns on whether one accepts that
formed-layer recognition source as the correct structural input,
not on a claimed classical proof of Weil positivity, a spectral
operator, or a de Branges-space positivity theorem.

\paragraph{Realisability stratum of the conclusion.}
The audit-closure framing of \citet{TsiokosAOR2026} fixes the
stratum at which the conditional landing chain delivers its
conclusion.  The eight-stratum / twelve-type residual atlas of the
AOR meta-theory partitions every audit obligation that a formed-layer
closure must discharge to count as a refinement-stable realisability
instance; in the present setting the seven discharge atoms named in
\Cref{def:rh:aor-sel-instance} are precisely the audit obligations
incurred by the recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), the bridge
theorem \Cref{thm:rh:dc-master-applied}, and the translation theorem
T, and the AOR-instance theorem records that no residue remains
outside the deferred-by-construction discharges named there.  The
realisability-instance form is therefore not a separate result
about \(\Sel\) but the audit-stratum certificate of the same
conditional landing chain; the critical-line statement on \(\Znt\)
is its typed-zero-ledger readout.  Within the Lean development this
certificate is recorded as partial because its Lean conclusion is
the local syntactic membership predicate, not the full audit-closure
characterisation.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Three extension targets are natural.  First, the admissibility of
\(\Sel\) should be derived rather than carried as construction-time
audit data.  A stronger development would unfold the seven
Foundations-II schemas as predicates and verify them for the
completed Selberg trace instance, replacing the construction-time
hypothesis by a derived formed-layer admissibility theorem; this
would not change Theorem T or the conditional landing chain.

Second, the real-coordinate parameter identification should be
formalized at the analytic level.  A stronger development would
connect the typed zero ledger \(\Znt\) and its real-coordinate
field to the nontrivial zeros of \(\zeta\) through
analytic-number-theoretic infrastructure, lifting the real-part
abstraction boundary disclosed in \Cref{sec:involution_and_ledger}.

Third, the natural generalization is to primitive Selberg-class
\(L\)-functions.  The functional-equation involution, completed
\(L\)-function, nontrivial-zero ledger, and anti-invariant ledger
all have evident analogues, but the analogy is not itself a
theorem: each \(L\) needs its own typed shell, admissibility
verification, and recognition-source provenance argument.  The
resulting statement is a per-\(L\) conditional landing chain for
the corresponding generalized Riemann hypothesis, not an automatic
GRH theorem obtained by changing notation.

The admissibility derivation and the real-coordinate identification
are closest to the existing formalization; the GRH extension is
structurally specified by the SDTC pattern, but its substantive
realization is a per-\(L\) development problem.
 

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The paper has established the RH closure in its conditional form.
If \(\Sel\) is a saturated completed Selberg trace closure
satisfying the standing Foundations-II admissibility assumption, and
if the Selberg-class recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is
supplied by the SDTC structural law of \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}, then
the conditional landing chain certifies the shell as a
refinement-stable audited-operational-realisability instance in the
sense of \citet{TsiokosAOR2026},
\[
  \Sel\in\mathrm{RefStableAOR}^{\mathrm{ref}}_{S_{\mathrm{RH}}},
\]
of which the critical-line statement on the typed zero ledger
\(\Znt\) is the readout:
\[
  \rho\in\Znt \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Real(\rho)=\frac12 .
\]
The proof is the two-step landing chain developed above.  The
recognition source supplies domination records for \(\AZ\); the
duality-confinement membrane theorem of \citet{TsiokosSDTC2026}
converts those records into \(\AZ=0\); translation theorem T
converts \(\AZ=0\) into the critical-line statement on the typed
zero ledger; and the AOR-instance theorem
(\Cref{thm:rh:aor-instance}) certifies that the same composition
discharges every atom of the eight-stratum / twelve-type residual
atlas of the audit-closure meta-theory, with the seven discharge
records assembled literally from the shell, the recognition source,
and the bridge fields.

The conditional structure is essential.  Outside the Six Birds
framework, the result reads as the implication
\[
  \GamSDTCSelberg \Longrightarrow \mathrm{RH}
\]
on the typed zero ledger, with \(\mathrm{RH}\) delivered as the
typed-ledger readout of the audit-closed realisability instance.
It is not an unconditional proof of RH in standard ZFC.  Acceptance
turns on two supplied pieces of formed-layer structure: the
recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\), which is closure content of
the formed self-dual Selberg trace layer, and the admissibility of
\(\Sel\), recorded by the \(\mathrm{Audit}_L\) field of the shell.
The theorem is therefore sharp as a conditional theorem; the
AOR-realisability framing names the audit-closure stratum at which
the closure delivers its conclusion, but does not collapse these
hypotheses into unmentioned background assumptions.  In the Lean
development the realisability-instance theorem is recorded as
partial because its conclusion is the local syntactic membership
predicate over the carrier fields rather than the full audit-closure
characterisation; the substantive recognition-source and
translation-interface discharges remain deferred to
\citet{TsiokosSDTC2026} and \Cref{sec:translation_theorem}.

Three extension targets remain.  First, a substantive Lean
derivation of the admissibility of \(\Sel\) from the seven
Foundations-II schemas would replace the construction-time audit
field by derived predicates.  Second, the typed real-coordinate
carrier should be connected inside the formal system to the
classical function \(\Real(\rho)\) through analytic-number-theoretic
infrastructure, lifting the real-part abstraction disclosed earlier.
Third, the analogous program for primitive Selberg-class
\(L\)-functions would require a separate typed shell, admissibility
verification, and recognition-source provenance argument for each
\(L\).  The paper accordingly defers unconditional standard-ZFC RH,
any claim to circumvent classical RH attack barriers in their native
sense, and GRH for general primitive \(L\)-functions.

\subsection*{Typed positive-cone encoding}

The formalization uses the same mathlib-free typed positive-cone
interface as \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}.  The interface records exactly
the primitives used by the arguments: an order \(\preceq\), a trace
functional \(\trace\), trace monotonicity under the order,
nonnegativity of the relevant scalar summands, and the
positivity-to-zero principles that turn vanishing trace or vanishing
square into vanishing obstruction.  No full Hilbert-space operator
theory is imported; the point of the encoding is to isolate the
trace-order content needed by the duality-confinement membrane
theorem and by the RH translation argument.

In the present paper this typed cone is the substrate on which the
anti-invariant zero ledger \(\AZ\) lives.  Theorem~T is a finite
typed-multiset argument over the cone: \(\AZ\) is the
multiplicity-weighted sum of squared anti-invariant readout values,
so \(\AZ=0\) is equivalent to every typed zero lying on the
critical line.  The bridge theorem \texttt{dcMasterApplied} then
applies the master theorem of \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026} to this
RH-specific typed cone.  A full operator-theoretic realization over
trace-class operators \citep{Simon2005} or over the classical
Selberg trace formula apparatus \citep{Hejhal1976,Hejhal1983} is an
extension target, not a hidden claim of the Lean development.

\subsection*{Representation note A3: real-part coordinate}

The RH formalization contains one representation note specific to the
zero ledger.  The paper definition of \(\AZ\) uses the classical
expression \(\Real(\rho)-1/2\).  In the mathlib-free Lean
development, however, the real-part coordinate is parameterized by a
typed carrier \texttt{RealCoordinate}, not by the standard
\(\mathbb{R}\) with analytic-number-theoretic infrastructure.  The
carrier supplies the algebraic structure needed for the proof:
\(1/2\), subtraction from \(1/2\), squaring, nonnegativity of squares,
and the implication that a zero square displacement forces equality
with \(1/2\).

The identification of \texttt{RealCoordinate} with the actual
\(\mathbb{R}\)-valued real part of a classical nontrivial zero of
\(\zeta\) is made by construction-parameter assignment when the
shell \(\Sel\) is instantiated.  It is not a derived Lean predicate.
This is forced by the mathlib-free constraint: the development does
not import the standard real-analysis and complex-analytic
infrastructure needed to prove inside Lean that the typed coordinate
equals the classical real-part function on zeta zeros.  The body
discloses this point in the ledger section; the present appendix
records the encoding rationale.  A substantive Lean derivation of
this identification is one of the open extensions named in
\Cref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection*{Recognition-source carrier (typed bridge)}

The recognition source \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is the distinctive
formal object of the RH paper.  It is realized in Lean as a typed
structure carrier \texttt{GammaSdtcSelberg} that packages, in one

\subsection*{No-smuggling gates}

The final audit layer is paper-side discipline that checks the
conditional theorem says only what its hypotheses support, with no
structural input hidden in the prose.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,labelsep=0.75em,itemsep=0.35em]
\item[Gate 1: no overreading.]
The conclusion is conditional on \(\GamSDTCSelberg\) and on
Foundations-II admissibility of \(\Sel\).  The typed-ledger
conclusion is exactly the parameterized statement
\(\rho\in\Znt \Rightarrow \Real(\rho)=1/2\).

\item[Gate 2: no undisclosed hypothesis.]
Every load-bearing assumption is named: the recognition source
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\), the \(\mathrm{Audit}_L\) admissibility field,
the typed real-coordinate identification, and the typed positive-cone
primitives.

\item[Gate 3: no hidden mechanism.]
The proof composition is explicit: the recognition source supplies
domination records, \Cref{thm:rh:dc-master-applied} yields
\(\AZ=0\), and \Cref{thm:rh:translation-T-forward} yields the
critical-line statement on \(\Znt\).

\item[Gate 4: no conflation of grades.]
Recognition-source content and theorem-grade content are separated.
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is a recognition source; Theorem~T is a direct
positivity theorem; \texttt{dcMasterApplied} is the theorem-level
application of the master theorem of \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026}; and
\texttt{rhConditional} is the theorem-grade conditional
composition.

\item[Gate 5: no axiom smuggling.]
\(\GamSDTCSelberg\) is a typed structure carrier, not a Lean axiom.
The forbidden-tokens audit excludes \texttt{axiom}, \texttt{opaque},
\texttt{constant}, \texttt{sorry}, and \texttt{admit} from active
Lean source.

\item[Gate 6: no undisclosed cross-paper import.]
The use of \cite{TsiokosSDTC2026} is explicit: the body reproduces
the duality-confinement master theorem statement, and
\texttt{dcMasterApplied} is the named Lean-side cross-paper import.
