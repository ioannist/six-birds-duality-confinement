\section*{Step 280: BSD Score-2 Audit of Skinner--Urban}

\paragraph{Target.}
The step audits whether Skinner--Urban's 2014 theorem on the Iwasawa main conjectures for \(\mathrm{GL}_2\) supplies the BSD b-52a cascade's p-adic bridge in the form required by the framework.

\paragraph{Published theorem scope.}
The paper states that it proves the Iwasawa--Greenberg main conjectures for ``a large class of modular forms that are ordinary with respect to an odd prime \(p\).''  The ordinary hypothesis is stated as \(a(p,f)\) being a \(p\)-adic unit.  Theorem 1 also assumes residual irreducibility, an auxiliary ramified prime \(q \ne p\) with \(q\Vert N\), and \(p\nmid N\).  Its conclusion is the characteristic ideal equality
\[
  \mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbf Q_\infty,L}(f)=(L_f)
\]
over the relevant Iwasawa algebra after rationalization, with an additional big-image condition for the integral equality.

\paragraph{BSD cascade need.}
The b-52a residual requires closure of the p-adic and determinant components in the full BSD package:
\[
  E_{\mathrm{an}}/\Omega + E_{\mathrm{ht}}/\mathrm{Reg} + E_{\mathrm{finite}}
  + \sum_p E_p + E_{\det}=0.
\]
This is an all-primes/all-cases demand.  It includes non-ordinary primes, bad-prime local components, residually reducible cases, missing auxiliary ramification cases, and determinant-line promotion from the Iwasawa statement to the global Bloch--Kato/ETNC residual.

\paragraph{Gap assessment.}
Skinner--Urban gives a powerful ordinary \(p\)-adic Iwasawa theorem.  It does not directly imply the all-primes b-52a bridge.  The missing content is not an algebraic corollary of the published theorem; it is a new all-cases bridge theorem.

  \boxed{V_{\mathrm{skinner\_urban\_BSD\_bridge\_blocked}}}

The BSD score-2 bridge candidate downgrades to score-1 for full b-52a closure.  Together with steps 267, 268, and 279, this gives a 4-of-4 empirical downgrade pattern across RH and BSD score-2 interfaces.
