\section{Purpose}
This note executes the first construction step of the hybrid semilocal RH program.  We now treat the archimedean/gamma slot as an imported Connes--Consani positivity record and focus on the finite-place interaction that remains when prime-place features are inserted into the semilocal Hardy--Titchmarsh carrier.

The goal is not to prove positivity.  The goal is to put all objects on one response core and define the actual residual form
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_S
=
q_{\Weil,S}-q_{\Pos,S}.
}
\]
This is the semilocal cross-term operator.  If its positive part is zero, defect-budgeted, or absorbed by a Hecke/Dirichlet source lower frame, then the Weil--Douglas bridge can advance.  If not, the route is support-only.

\section{Semilocal response core}
Let $S$ be a finite set of rational places with $\infty\in S$.  Write $S_f=S\setminus\{\infty\}$.  The semilocal adele class space is
\[
X_S=A_S/\Gamma,
\qquad
\Gamma=\{\pm p_1^{n_1}\cdots p_k^{n_k}:p_i\in S_f,\ n_i\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
The semilocal Hardy--Titchmarsh map sends a real even function $f$ to a $K_S$-invariant semilocal vector $\eta_S(f)\in L^2(X_S)^{K_S}$, and on the Mellin/Fourier side it inserts finite local Euler factors:
\[
F_\mu w_S(\eta_S f)(\xi)
= L_{S_f}(1/2-i\xi)\,F_\mu w_\infty f(\xi),
\qquad
L_{S_f}(z):=\prod_{p\in S_f}(1-p^{-z})^{-1}.
\]
The canonical semilocal spectral response space is
\[
Y_S:=L^2(\R,dm_S),
\qquad
 dm_S(\xi)=|L_\infty(1/2-i\xi)L_{S_f}(1/2-i\xi)|^2d\xi,
\]
with canonical unitary $V_S=M_SU_S$.

The anti-invariant response sector is denoted $Y_S^-$.  The core used in this note is
\[
\boxed{
\CS_S^-:=V_S\eta_S(\mathcal S_0(\R)^{\mathrm{ev}})\cap Y_S^-.
}
\]
Here $\mathcal S_0$ records the usual zero/pole vanishing conditions required by the explicit formula.  A later proof must replace this core-level object by a full completed response space plus a closability/core-extension record.

\section{Actual semilocal Weil form}
Let $QW_S$ denote the semilocal Weil quadratic form on $L^2(X_S)^{K_S}$ or its compact-support form core.  Pull it to the response side:
\[
\boxed{
q_{\Weil,S}[y]
:=QW_S(V_S^{-1}y,V_S^{-1}y),
\qquad y\in\CS_S^-.
}
\]
This form is the target signed/semibounded object.  It is not automatically a positive feature package.

At finite support/cutoff, $QW_S$ should decompose into the standard pieces
\[
q_{\Weil,S}
= q_{0,2,S}^{\mathrm{sgn}}
-q_{\infty,S}^{\mathrm{sgn}}
-\sum_{p\in S_f}q_{p,S}^{\mathrm{sgn}}
+q_{\tail,S}^{\mathrm{sgn}},
\]
with convention-dependent signs.  The signs themselves are not the proof object; the carrier proof object is the domination inequality obtained after positive packaging.

\section{Positive imported/candidate package}
The hybrid route imports the archimedean positive piece from the Connes--Consani Sonin/prolate compression theorem.  Write it as
\[
q_\infty^{\cc}[y]
=\|W_\infty^{\cc}y\|^2
\]
where its domain, support, vanishing, finite-codimension correction, and Sonin/prolate records are part of the archimedean gate.

For finite primes we use a paired candidate, not separated diagonal accounting:
\[
q_{\pr,S}^{\mathrm{pair}}[y]
=
\sum_{p\in S_f}\sum_{k\ge1}^{\mathrm{visible}}
(\log p)p^{-k/2}\,
\|\tau_{k\log p}R_{\log}y-R_{\log}y\|^2.
\]
Here $R_{\log}$ is the response-to-log-core bridge.  In a full proof it must be declared as part of the semilocal carrier.  The formula is a paired CND feature template, not a claim that the signed explicit formula has already been matched.

Add pole/completion and tail slots:
\[
q_{\Pos,S}[y]
:=q_\infty^{\cc}[y]+q_{\pr,S}^{\mathrm{pair}}[y]+q_{\pole,S}^+[y]+q_{\tail,S}^+[y].
\]
The pole slot is finite-rank and can only pay pole/null/endpoint modes.  The tail slot records finite-prime omissions, support leakage, and finite-to-completed promotion defects.

\section{Definition of the semilocal cross-term}
\begin{definition}[Semilocal cross-term operator]
On the common form core $\CS_S^-$ define
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_S[y]
:=q_{\Weil,S}[y]-q_{\Pos,S}[y].
}
\]
Equivalently, if the forms are represented by self-adjoint form operators on a common Hilbert space,
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_S=A_{\Weil,S}-A_{\Pos,S}.
}
\]
The positive part $\Delta_S^+$ is the obstruction to replacing the signed semilocal Weil form by the positive feature package.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cross-term extraction gate]
Suppose on $\CS_S^-$ the zero-side form satisfies
\[
q_Z[y]\le q_{\Weil,S}[y]+e_{\Weil,S}[y],
\]
and the semilocal cross-term satisfies
\[
\Delta_S[y]\le e_{\Delta,S}[y].
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
q_Z[y]\le q_{\Pos,S}[y]+e_{\Weil,S}[y]+e_{\Delta,S}[y].
}
\]
If both defects vanish and the core-to-full Douglas gate passes, then
\[
V_Z^*V_Z\preceq W_{\Pos,S}^*W_{\Pos,S},
\]
so there is a contraction
\[
V_Z=T W_{\Pos,S},
\qquad \|T\|\le1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By definition,
\[
q_{\Weil,S}=q_{\Pos,S}+\Delta_S.
\]
Substitute this identity into the first inequality and bound $\Delta_S$ by $e_{\Delta,S}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Source absorption of the cross-term}
The source-coercivity route does not require $\Delta_S\le0$.  It requires the positive part of $\Delta_S$ to be paid by upstream-visible source records.

A source family $\mathcal S_n$ supplies a response-side frame
\[
F_n=
\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_n}
\lambda_s Q_s^*\Theta_s^{-1}Q_s.
\]
The absorption gate is
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_S^+\preceq F_n+E_{\mathrm{abs},n}.
}
\]
If in addition
\[
F_n\succeq \Lambda_n(\Theta_0^-)^{-1},
\qquad
\Lambda_n\to\infty,
\]
then the positive cross-term is not merely bounded; it is converted into anti-invariant currency collapse, provided the source records are upstream-visible and exhaustive.

\section{What $\Delta_S$ contains}
The extracted cross-term is not one scalar.  It is a ledger of mismatches:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Semilocal measure deformation.}  The response measure changes from the archimedean measure to
\[
 dm_S=|L_\infty(1/2-i\xi)|^2|L_{S_f}(1/2-i\xi)|^2d\xi.
\]
This changes orthogonal polynomials, the number/grading operator, and the prolate/Sonin decomposition.

\item \textbf{Non-unitary semilocal injection.}  The map $\eta_S$ inserts finite local Euler factors but is not unitary in the archimedean response geometry.  Hence semilocal grading and archimedean grading do not commute in general.

\item \textbf{Prime--archimedean cross-term.}  The finite local factors enter multiplicatively in the Hardy--Titchmarsh transform, while the candidate positive package is additive in feature norms.  The gap between multiplicative insertion and additive feature spending is the heart of $\Delta_S$.

\item \textbf{Pole/completion modes.}  Pole, endpoint, and null-mode constraints must be recorded as finite-rank positive features or quotient data.

\item \textbf{Tail/promotion defects.}  A finite $S$ statement is exact only at finite semilocal level.  Full RH requires a fixed-ledger or exhaustive-ledger squeeze as $S\nearrow\mathcal P$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conrey--Li survival note}
The extracted $\Delta_S$ gate must not collapse into a de Branges/RKHS shift-positivity ansatz.  The hybrid route is semilocal, paired, source-aware, and adequacy-aware.  If it reduces to a condition of the form $\Re\langle F(z),F(z+i)\rangle>0$ in a zeta RKHS, it inherits the Conrey--Li obstruction and should be abandoned or radically rephrased.

\section{Conclusion}
Step 95 gives the first concrete analytic object of the hybrid program:
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_S=q_{\Weil,S}-q_{\Pos,S}.
}
\]
The next RH construction task is no longer vague.  For finite $S$, compute or estimate this form on the semilocal response core.  Then prove one of:
\[
\Delta_S\preceq0,
\qquad
\Delta_S\preceq E_S\to0,
\qquad
\Delta_S^+\preceq F_n+E_n
\text{ with source-frame collapse.}
\]
Without one of these, the hybrid semilocal route remains support-only.
