# Step 447 Literature Anchor

## Conrey 2003

Conrey, "The Riemann Hypothesis", Notices AMS 50(3), is cited for the Hilbert-Polya program's central gap: no arithmetic-independent Hermitian operator construction is known that produces the zeta-zero ordinates pointwise. In this step's terms, pointwise RH-distinguishing content must be accounted for by an audit-bearing mechanism.

## Bombieri 2000

Bombieri, "The Riemann Hypothesis" (Clay Millennium Problem statement), is cited for the same structural gap: Hilbert-Polya remains a hopeful direction, but the missing content is an actual construction, not merely an analogy or statistical match.

## Lagarias 2004

Lagarias, "Hilbert spaces of entire functions and the Riemann hypothesis", is cited for the de Branges obstruction history. In this step's terms, the structure function route faces the concrete/formal split: concrete `xi` imports the target; abstract structure does not deliver the needed positivity.

## de Branges 1968

de Branges, "Hilbert Spaces of Entire Functions", is cited for the Hilbert-space-of-entire-functions framework underlying the kernel-positivity audit class tested in step 442.

## Weil 1952

Weil, "Sur les 'formules explicites' de la theorie des nombres premiers", is cited for explicit-formula positivity: concrete evaluation of the form uses the zero side or prime side, exactly the audit-content dichotomy tested in step 443.

## NDO / SAU Shape, Verbatim

> An audited descending expression is the return leg of a promoted computation. It says that, under a specific promotion bridge, the expression `C(U)` formed from the promoted datum `U` descends to a lower answer `a^sharp`, and that the bridge checks licensing this return have passed.

> The certificate pattern can be read in its shortest form as
> `[U not_down_q, C(U) down_B a^sharp]`.

The SAU anchor supports the step's structural claim: audit content must be supplied by a verified descent, not by naming the desired target.
