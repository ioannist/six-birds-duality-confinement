# Step 447 Theorem Statement and Grade Calibration

## Definitions

A **finite typed package on the L-function substrate** is a finite collection of typed objects whose primitives are declared in operator-theoretic / functional-analytic / probe-family vocabulary on an L-function legal-zero-set carrier. In the covered constitutive-closure cases, the objects carry Loewner-currency, Schur-complement, adequacy-residual, or Capacity-bound types.

A package is **intended to imply zero-localization without external audit** when its closure conclusion, whether a typed predicate, residual-vanishing claim, Capacity bound, or sub-component interpretation, is intended to be RH-on-scope-equivalent by itself.

**Audit-content** is any sub-object, semantic interpretation, or meaning function inside the package whose role is to bridge non-arithmetic primitive-level data to the RH-distinguishing closure conclusion. It may be a separate typed component, the semantics of `K^D <= Theta^D`, or the interpretation of an adequacy/residual/transport sub-component.

## Covered-Class Theorem

For finite constitutive-closure style packages on the L-function substrate with audit-content placed as (i) a separate typed component, (ii) Capacity-bound semantics, or (iii) sub-component interpretation of `K^D`, `Theta^D`, `Xi`, or transport output, the following dichotomy holds:

1. If the audit-content is concrete and RH-distinguishing, it imports arithmetic content: zero heights, prime/von-Mangoldt data, the target function `xi`, or an RH-equivalent semantic.
2. If the audit-content is formal and non-smuggling, it lacks enough evaluation content to derive a numerical adequacy residual, Capacity-bound RH distinction, or closure conclusion.

Therefore, no audit-content-free constitutive-closure construction at the L-function substrate exists in the covered placement classes.

## Grade Calibration

**Theorem-grade:** the covered placement classes above. They are demonstrated by steps 441-445 and consolidated by step 446.

**Diagnosis-grade:** the unrestricted universal claim over every conceivable typed predicate, semantic interpretation, or future placement. The class of all possible audit-content placements is not a closed mathematical category here, so a literal universal quantifier would overclaim.
