                                                 SCATTERING FOR TIME SERIES WITH AN
                                               APPLICATION TO THE ZETA FUNCTION OF AN
                                                          ALGEBRAIC CURVE


                                                                       Jean-François Burnol

                                                                              November 1999




                                             I explain how the Lax-Phillips theory can be applied to a purely innovating
                                             time series and compute the corresponding scattering function. I then as-
                                             sociate such a time series to an algebraic curve (of genus at least 1) over a
                                             finite field and show that the Riemann Hypothesis (proven long ago) holds
                                             if and only if the scattering is causal (this causality is not independently
                                             established, though).

Let us first briefly review some concepts related to time series. See for exam-
ple [GreRo57] for this as well as [DymKe72] and [Ho62] for the underlying
key results of classical harmonic analysis (Herglotz’s theorem, Hardy spaces,
Szegö’s theorem, inner and outer factors, the Beurling–Lax description of
invariant subspaces, etc. . . ) which we will use below.


Let X = (Xn )n∈Z be a sequence of square-integrable random variables
on some probability space, with zero mean, and such that the covariance
E(Xn Xm ) depends only on the difference n − m (and is denoted γn−m ). We
also assume that γ0 > 0.


The γj ’s satisfy the positivity condition of Herglotz and are thus the Fourier
coefficients of a uniquely determined positive measure µ on the circle S 1 :
γj = z −j dµ (0 < µ(S 1 ) < ∞), the so-called “spectral measure” of the

Let H(X) (the history of X) be the Hilbert space spanned by the Xn ’s.
There is a unique isometry L2 (S 1 , dµ) → H(X) which sends the function
z n to the random variable Xn . Multiplication by z then provides a (unique)
isometry U of H(X) such that U (Xn ) = Xn+1 . We have a chain of increasing
subspaces Hn ⊂ Hn+1 with Hn spanned by the Xm ’s, m ≤ n. Let H−∞ be
the intersection of all the Hn ’s (the distant past of the process X). Then
either H−∞ = H(X), or all inclusions Hn ⊂ Hn+1 are strict. If H−∞ =
{0} we are necessarily in this second case and we will say that the time
series X is “purely innovating” (the traditional terminology is “purely non-
deterministic”). It then has no distant past, and no distant future either as
