                                                                                 JEAN-FRANÇOIS BURNOL


                                                   Abstract. We represent the Riemann zeta function in the half-plane ℜs > 1 via series
                                                   whose terms admit geometrically decreasing bounds. Due to an underlying recurrence
                                                   relation, which is used to compute coeﬃcients entering into the terms, the cost is probably
                                                   at least quadratic in the number of terms. And the number of terms needed to reach a
                                                   given ﬁxed-point precision grows linearly with the imaginary part, so, presumably, the

                                                   usefulness is limited to small imaginary parts (up to the hundreds perhaps). The method
                                                   is a development of tools introduced by the author for the evaluation of harmonic series
                                                   with restricted digits in a given radix.

    The paper is organized as follows. In the ﬁrst section, we extend our earlier framework
[4], which involves certain measures and moments tailored to compute harmonic series with
restrictions on the allowed digits. For a given radix b and choice of admissible digits, we
develop the apparatus to compute the associated Dirichlet series not only at s = 1 but
everywhere in the half-plane of convergence (such “restricted” Dirichlet series have been
considered earlier [1, 9, 11]).

   In the second section we study the exponential moment generating function, and establish
some of its analytic properties. These analytic aspects are related to an old theme going
back all the way to Hardy [2, 7, 8, 10, 12, 13].
   In the ﬁnal section, we remove all restrictions on the radix-b digits, so that we are comput-
ing the Riemann zeta function ζ(s), ℜs > 1. From the point of view of obtaining “explicit”
formulas, the removal of all restrictions brings a substantial theoretical simpliﬁcation and
allows to express the series coeﬃcients in terms of Bernoulli numbers as is shown in equation
(3) for b = 2. But, as pointed out already, these “explicit” formulas are mostly of theoretical
interest, the recurrence (1) being numerically much more eﬃcient than equation (3), despite
having roughly twice as many terms.

                         2. Measures on words, and moments
   Let b > 1 an integer. Let W be the space of words (also called ﬁnite strings) on the
alphabet D = [[0, b − 1]] of the b-ary digits. The empty word is noted ǫ. The length of a word
w is noted ℓ(w). The power notation wj means concatenation (w2 = ww, etc. . . ). And
w0 = ǫ.
Deﬁnition 1. For w = d1 . . . dl ∈ W we say that w “represents” the integer n(w) =
d1 bl−1 + · · · + dl ∈ N. In particular n(ǫ) = 0. We deﬁne x(w) = n(w)/bℓ(w) . In other terms,
x(w) is obtained in b-radix representation from positioning the word w immediately to the
right of the radix separator. It belongs to [0, 1).
    For n a non-negative integer, l = ℓ(n) is the length of the minimal-length representation
of n by a word, i.e. it is the smallest non-negative integer such that n < bl .
    Let A be some subset of D. Its elements will be called the “admissible” digits, and
words using only them are said to be “admissible”. The admissible integers are those whose
minimal b-representation is an admissible word. So 0 is always an admissible integer, but is
not necessarily an admissible digit.
   Throughout this section it is assumed that the set A of admissible digits is neither empty
nor reduced to the singleton {0}. So the set x(W) contains positive elements. Contrarily to
[4] it is now allowed for A to be the full alphabet of digits. We let N be the cardinality of
A and N1 the one of A \ {0}. So 0 < N1 ≤ N ≤ b.
Deﬁnition 2. Let s ∈ R. We deﬁne a positive measure µA,s on W.
