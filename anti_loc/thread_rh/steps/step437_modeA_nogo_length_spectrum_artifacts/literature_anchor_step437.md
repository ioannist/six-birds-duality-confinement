# Step 437 Literature Anchor

## Margulis prime geodesic theorem

For compact negatively curved manifolds, Margulis proves the primitive closed-geodesic counting law

```text
pi_X(T) ~ exp(hT)/(hT),
```

where `h` is the topological entropy of the geodesic flow. This supplies the formal leading-term analogy with the prime number theorem after setting `x=exp T` and rescaling to `h=1`.

## Sarnak refinements and Selberg context

Sarnak's work around Selberg trace formula and arithmetic hyperbolic surfaces is the relevant refinement context: arithmetic surfaces have trace formulas whose geometric side is not merely a generic length spectrum but a length spectrum constrained by arithmetic structure.

## Pollicott-Ruelle resonances

For Anosov flows and related hyperbolic dynamics, orbit-counting errors and correlation decay are governed by Pollicott-Ruelle resonances / transfer-operator spectral data. These are dynamical invariants of the flow, independent of Riemann-zero arithmetic unless imposed by extra structure.

## Lafont-McReynolds 2014

Lafont-McReynolds, Publ. Mat. 2014, prove that every noncompact arithmetic hyperbolic 2- or 3-manifold has arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions in its primitive length spectrum. This is cited as evidence of a structural arithmetic/non-arithmetic dichotomy in length spectra.

## Sunada

Sunada's method constructs isospectral non-isometric manifolds. This shows that length spectra alone need not determine the geometric substrate, so a no-go argument cannot rely on length-spectrum rigidity alone. It must instead rely on the absence of a natural arithmetic mechanism producing `{log p}` and the mismatch of refined trace-error structure.
