# Step 41 — Formed-Layer Membrane Theorem

This step assembles the static, predictive, family, protocol, and critical-pair components into one theorem.

## Main theorem

For a formed closure/layer with exact packaged carriers, native predictive probe family, response transports, and legal null-mode records, define

\[
\widehat K_j=R_jL_j\Gamma_j(\Gamma_j^*C_j\Gamma_j)^\dagger\Gamma_j^*L_j^*R_j^*.
\]

Then the following are equivalent:

1. Predictive family currency bound:
   \[
   \widehat K_j\preceq\Theta\quad\text{for all accepted }j.
   \]
2. No native predictive recombination needle:
   \[
   \sup_j y^*\widehat K_jy\le y^*\Theta y\quad\forall y.
   \]
3. No unresolved native critical-pair witness:
   \[
   \nexists (j,y):y^*(\widehat K_j-\Theta)y>0.
   \]
4. Completed critical-pair ledger: every in-scope native witness is priced, repaired, or explicitly excluded by nonclaim.

When the Six Birds gates are accepted, these equivalent conditions are the accepted anti-localization membrane of the formed layer.

## Philosophical reading

A formed layer is not just a stable package. It has a membrane when every native predictive way of addressing it has a finite declared price. Needles in non-closed artifacts are not counterexamples because the theorem is scoped to formed closures/layers.

## Key nonclaims

The theorem does not assert that raw substrates have no needles, that undeclared probes are priced, that public shadows are lawful witnesses, or that RH/Navier are solved.
