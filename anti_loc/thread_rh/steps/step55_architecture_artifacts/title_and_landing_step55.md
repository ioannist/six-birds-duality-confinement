**Formed-Layer Membrane Theory: Anti-Localization, Adequacy, and the Root-Composite Obligation**

The theory has grown beyond anti-localization as a scalar/probe estimate. Anti-localization is now one consequence of a broader membrane framework:

- native probe-family currency \(\mathsf K\);
- adequacy residual \(\Xi_C(D\mid L)\);
- predictive transport;
- critical-pair completion;
- exact-six records;
- transfer and type-promotion;
- root-composite zero-confinement obligations.

## The landing paragraph

A formed layer is not certified by a single scalar capacity bound, a public shadow, a finite-stage check, or a route-local calculation. It is certified by a predictive native probe-family currency budget, an adequacy residual record for layer-dissolving probes, a completed critical-pair ledger, lawful transfer/promotion records, and all-six channel statuses. Anti-localization is the no-cheap-native-needle theorem inside this membrane theory. The object \(\Xi_C(D\mid L)\) is the blind-spot currency measuring what the layer does not know about its own possible dissolution.
