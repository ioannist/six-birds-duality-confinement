\section{Purpose}

Step 88 showed that the low-frequency cancellation route is viable only if the zero-side readout admits a uniform primitive factorization.  This note audits the other serious route: source coercivity.  The target is not to prove RH.  The target is to say exactly what a lawful strict-extension source ladder must do in order to charge the low-frequency anti-invariant sector of the paired completed Weil carrier.

The paired carrier has a legal zero at log-frequency zero:
\[
  \Psi_{\rm pair}(0)=0.
\]
Therefore positivity of the paired prime/gamma feature package is not enough.  A source-coercivity ladder must provide an upstream-visible lower frame on the anti-invariant response family.

\section{Response-space setup}

Let \(\Y\) be the anti-invariant response space.  Let
\[
  L^-_\Gamma:E\to \Y
\]
be the anti-invariant probe family on the exact packaged carrier and let
\[
  C_{\rm pair}\succeq 0
\]
be the paired completed Weil carrier candidate.  We assume null-mode legality throughout; if a declared probe sees a legal null direction, capacity is infinite and the source-coercivity claim is not accepted.

A source record is a tuple
\[
  s=(Q_s,\Theta_s,\lambda_s,D_s),
\]
where \(Q_s:\Y\to Z_s\) is an upstream-visible readout, \(\Theta_s\succ0\) is the source response budget, \(\lambda_s>0\) is source strength, and
\[
  D_s\succeq \lambda_s (Q_s L^-_\Gamma)^*\Theta_s^{-1}(Q_sL^-_\Gamma).
\]
The total source audit is
\[
  D_{\mathcal S}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}D_s,
\qquad
  C^+ = C_{\rm pair}+D_{\mathcal S}.
\]
The response-side source-frame operator is
\[
  \F_{\mathcal S}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\lambda_s Q_s^*\Theta_s^{-1}Q_s.
\]

\begin{theorem}[source-frame coercivity]
If
\[
  \F_{\mathcal S}\succeq \Lambda (\Thetaop)^{-1},
\]
then
\[
  C^+\succeq \Lambda (L^-_\Gamma)^*(\Thetaop)^{-1}L^-_\Gamma.
\]
Consequently, under null-mode legality,
\[
  \K^-_+ := L^-_\Gamma (C^+)^\dagger (L^-_\Gamma)^*\preceq \Lambda^{-1}\Thetaop.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The source lower frame gives
\[
  \sum_sD_s\succeq (L^-_\Gamma)^*\F_{\mathcal S}L^-_\Gamma
  \succeq \Lambda (L^-_\Gamma)^*(\Thetaop)^{-1}L^-_\Gamma.
\]
Since \(C_{\rm pair}\succeq0\), the same lower bound holds for \(C^+\).  For every response vector \(y\), put \(g=(L^-_\Gamma)^*y\).  The lower bound implies
\[
  \langle u,C^+u\rangle\ge \Lambda\langle L^-_\Gamma u,(\Thetaop)^{-1}L^-_\Gamma u\rangle.
\]
By the variational capacity identity this yields
\[
  y^*\K^-_+y
  =\sup_{u\ne0}\frac{|\langle y,L^-_\Gamma u\rangle|^2}{\langle u,C^+u\rangle}
  \le \Lambda^{-1}y^*\Thetaop y.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Low-frequency specialization}

Suppose \(\Y\) admits a frequency decomposition
\[
  \Y=\int^\oplus \Y_\xi\,d\nu(\xi),
\]
and the paired carrier has symbol \(\Psi_{\rm pair}(\xi)\), with \(\Psi_{\rm pair}(0)=0\).  A source ladder is full-sector coercive if its response-frame field satisfies
\[
  \F_n(\xi)\succeq \Lambda_n \Theta_0(\xi)^{-1}
\quad\text{for a.e. }\xi,
\]
with \(\Lambda_n\to\infty\).  Then \(\K^-_n\preceq \Lambda_n^{-1}\Theta_0^-\to0\).

If instead the lower bound only holds on a low-frequency window \(|\xi|<\varepsilon_n\), then it resolves only that sector.  A full-RH confinement claim additionally needs either high-frequency carrier coercivity, finite-window exhaustivity, or a declared nonclaim/defect for the uncovered region.

\section{Character-source version}

If
\[
  \Y=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\widehat G^-}\Y_\chi
\]
is an anti-invariant character decomposition and \(P_\chi\) are the character projections, a character source ladder has
\[
  \F_n=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G^-}\lambda_{\chi,n}P_\chi^*\Theta_\chi^{-1}P_\chi.
\]
If the character family is orthogonal and \(\Theta_\chi\) is compatible with \(\Theta_0^-\), then
\[
  \Lambda_n=\min_{\chi\in\widehat G^-}\lambda_{\chi,n}.
\]
Thus full anti-invariant budget collapse requires
\[
  \min_{\chi\in\widehat G^-}\lambda_{\chi,n}\to\infty.
\]
If a nonzero character direction is missing, then \(\Lambda_n=0\) for all \(n\), and the source ladder cannot prove full-sector collapse.

\section{No-go alternatives}

\begin{proposition}[partial low-frequency source is not full collapse]
Let \(\Y=L^2([-1,1])\) and let
\[
  C_n(\xi)=\xi^2+\lambda_n\mathbf 1_{|\xi|<\epsilon}.
\]
Even if \(\lambda_n\to\infty\), the capacity of an arbitrary response family does not collapse on all of \(\Y\) unless the complement \(|\xi|\ge\epsilon\) is also controlled by the carrier, a tail bridge, or a nonclaim.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[missing character leaves a blind direction]
Let \(\Y=\mathbb R^2\), \(\Theta_0^-=I\), and
\[
  \F_n=\lambda_n e_1e_1^*.
\]
Then \(e_2\) is an uncovered anti-invariant direction and no lower frame
\[
  \F_n\succeq \Lambda_n I
\]
holds with \(\Lambda_n>0\).  The budget in the \(e_2\)-direction does not collapse.
\end{proposition}

\begin{warning}[upstream visibility]
A source chosen after observing the zero-side displacement or after optimizing the final Douglas inequality is not a lawful source.  It is a target-selected repair and is at best support-only.  The source ladder must be declared by upstream data: primes, characters, automorphic/trace-side structure, completion, or an explicit strict-extension rule.
\end{warning}

\section{RH-facing audit outcome}

The source-coercivity route is accepted only if it provides all of the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(S\arabic*)]
\item an upstream-visible source family \(\mathcal S_n\), not selected from the target zeros;
\item a lower frame \(\F_{\mathcal S,n}\succeq\Lambda_n(\Theta_0^-)^{-1}\);
\item \(\Lambda_n\to\infty\), or a fixed/exhaustive ledger squeeze with total obstruction trace tending to zero;
\item coverage of every anti-invariant character/frequency direction visible to the dissolving zero readout;
\item null-mode legality and all-six channel records;
\item defect accounting for low-frequency windows, tails, and noncovered sectors.
\end{enumerate}

Thus the source-coercivity route is structurally strong but analytically demanding.  Its load-bearing mathematical obligation is a full-sector lower frame from natural zeta-side source records.
