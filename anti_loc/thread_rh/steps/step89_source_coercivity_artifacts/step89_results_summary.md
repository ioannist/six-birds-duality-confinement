# Step 89: Zeta Source-Coercivity Audit

This step audits the second serious low-frequency route after the cancellation route from Step 88: a strict-extension source ladder that makes the paired Weil carrier coercive on the anti-invariant sector.

## Main theorem

Let `L^-_Gamma` be the anti-invariant probe family and let `C_pair` be the paired completed Weil carrier.  A source record is a positive audit contribution

\[
D_s \succeq \lambda_s (Q_sL^-_\Gamma)^*\Theta_s^{-1}(Q_sL^-_\Gamma).
\]

Define the response-side source frame

\[
F_{\mathcal S}=\sum_s\lambda_sQ_s^*\Theta_s^{-1}Q_s.
\]

If

\[
F_{\mathcal S}\succeq \Lambda(\Theta_0^-)^{-1},
\]

then

\[
K^-_+=L^-_\Gamma(C_{\rm pair}+\sum_sD_s)^\dagger(L^-_\Gamma)^*
\preceq \Lambda^{-1}\Theta_0^-.
\]

So source coercivity collapses the anti-invariant budget only if it gives a full-sector lower frame.

## RH meaning

The source family must be declared upstream: primes, characters, automorphic/trace-side structure, completion, or a strict-extension rule.  It cannot be selected after seeing where the zero-side displacement lives.

A full RH route needs either:

1. a full anti-invariant lower frame with `Lambda_n -> infinity`, or
2. a fixed/exhaustive ledger squeeze whose total obstruction trace tends to zero.

Partial source coverage, low-frequency-only coverage, or invariant/trace-only control is insufficient for full RH confinement.

## Countermodels

- A source that covers only one anti-invariant character leaves the other direction at nonzero capacity.
- A source that covers only a low-frequency window is scoped unless the complement is controlled by paired carrier coercivity, tail/exhaustivity, or a nonclaim record.
- A target-selected source is smuggled and cannot be accepted as an upstream source record.

## Bottom line

The source-coercivity route is structurally the strongest non-gating way to resolve the low-frequency zero, but it is analytically hard.  It requires a natural zeta-side source family that covers every anti-invariant character/frequency direction with lower-frame strength tending to infinity.
