\section*{Step 310: Candidate Lower-Bound Theorem for Branch C}

\subsection*{Inherited certified facts}

Let
\[
  \rho_1 = \frac12+i\gamma_1,\qquad \gamma_1 \approx 14.134725,
\]
and let \(G_\star\) be the compactly supported smooth test function on
\([1.3,3.7]\) with centers \(1.5,2.5,3.5\), \(\epsilon=0.20\), and
coefficients \(1,-3.3409952306,2.3409952306\).  The inherited Mellin
convention is
\[
  M(G)(z)=\int_0^\infty G(t)t^{-z}\,dt.
\]
Set
\[
  h(z)=\zeta(z)M(G_\star)(z),\qquad
  \delta_{D,k}(\rho_1,G_\star)=h^{(k)}(\rho_1).
\]

Step 307 certified:
\[
  |M(G_\star)(1)|=
  1.33943834915891599478412134567032143685770702518296718111148
  \times 10^{-102},
\]
so the pole of \(\zeta\) at \(1\) is numerically cancelled by
\(M(G_\star)(1)=0\).  Step 307 also certified
\[
  M(G_\star)(\rho_1)=-0.06965146358+0.18763300553i\neq 0,
\]
so the zero of \(h\) at \(\rho_1\) is inherited from the simple zero of
\(\zeta\).

Step 308 certified that \(M(G_\star)^{(m)}(\rho_1)\) is non-lacunary for
\(0\le m\le 30\), and that the Leibniz reconstruction matches the
certified magnitudes at \(k=10,20,30,50\) to displayed precision.

Step 309 computed the termwise Leibniz distribution
\[
  T_{k,j}
  =
  {k\choose j}\zeta^{(j)}(\rho_1)M(G_\star)^{(k-j)}(\rho_1),
  \qquad 1\le j\le k,
\]
and found a broad high-\(j\) saddle:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
k & j_\ast(k) & j_\ast(k)/k
  & |\sum_jT_{k,j}|/\sum_j|T_{k,j}|\\
\hline
10&4&0.40&0.8119400203\\
20&10&0.50&0.6254689266\\
30&15&0.50&0.4694197978\\
50&28&0.56&0.2684622890.
\end{array}
\]
A least-squares fit to these interference ratios gives
\[
  \frac{|\sum_jT_{k,j}|}{\sum_j|T_{k,j}|}
  \approx 1.07997\exp(-0.0277874\,k)
\]
on \(k=10,20,30,50\).  This is an empirical diagnostic, not yet a
rigorous bound.

\subsection*{Candidate theorem: certified-sample form}

\paragraph{Candidate Theorem 310.A (Branch C lower-bound form, sample-calibrated).}
For \(G_\star\) and \(\rho_1\) as above, define
\[
  \delta_{D,k}(\rho_1,G_\star)
  =
  \left(\zeta M(G_\star)\right)^{(k)}(\rho_1).
\]
Assume the two lower-bound lemmata stated below.  Then there exist
explicit constants
\[
  A_0=10^{-2},\qquad B_0=2.5,\qquad \alpha_0=0,\qquad k_0=10
\]
such that
\[
  |\delta_{D,k}(\rho_1,G_\star)|
  \ge A_0 B_0^k k^{\alpha_0}
  =
  10^{-2}\,2.5^k
  \qquad (k\ge k_0).
\]

\paragraph{Required Lemma 1: central-window magnitude lower bound.}
There are constants \(C_M>0\), \(B_M>0\), \(\beta\in\mathbb R\), and a
central interval \(J_k\subset \{1,\ldots,k\}\) of width \(\asymp\sqrt{k}\)
around \(j_\ast(k)\sim k/2\) such that
\[
  \sum_{j\in J_k}|T_{k,j}|
  \ge C_M B_M^k k^\beta
  \qquad(k\ge k_0).
\]
This is the rigorous version of the broad high-\(j\) saddle observed in
Step 309.

\paragraph{Required Lemma 2: phase/interference lower bound.}
There are constants \(C_I>0\) and \(\eta\ge0\), with the empirical
diagnostic \(\eta\approx0.0277874\), such that
\[
  \left|\sum_{j=1}^kT_{k,j}\right|
  \ge
  C_I e^{-\eta k}\sum_{j\in J_k}|T_{k,j}|
  \qquad(k\ge k_0).
\]
This is the load-bearing missing lemma.  It requires explicit control of
the phases of
\(\zeta^{(j)}(\rho_1)M(G_\star)^{(k-j)}(\rho_1)\) in the central saddle
window.

\subsection*{Proof sketch}

The pole-cancellation hypothesis \(M(G_\star)(1)=0\) makes
\(h=\zeta M(G_\star)\) entire.  Since \(M(G_\star)(\rho_1)\neq0\) and
\(\rho_1\) is a simple zero of \(\zeta\), \(h\) has a simple zero at
\(\rho_1\).

For every \(k\ge1\), Leibniz gives the identity
\[
  \delta_{D,k}
  =
  \sum_{j=0}^k {k\choose j}
  \zeta^{(j)}(\rho_1)M(G_\star)^{(k-j)}(\rho_1).
\]
The \(j=0\) term vanishes because \(\zeta(\rho_1)=0\).  Step 308
certifies that the \(M(G_\star)\)-derivative side is non-lacunary in the
computed range; Step 309 shows the resulting \(j\)-distribution has a
broad high-\(j\) saddle.

If Lemma 1 supplies an exponential lower bound on the magnitude in the
central saddle window, and Lemma 2 supplies a non-catastrophic
interference bound, then
\[
  |\delta_{D,k}|
  \ge C_I C_M k^\beta \exp((\log B_M-\eta)k).
\]
Choosing any \(B_0< B_Me^{-\eta}\) and absorbing finite initial values
into \(A_0\) gives the stated lower bound.  The sample-calibrated
constants \(A_0=10^{-2}\) and \(B_0=2.5\) are consistent with the certified
values at \(k=10,20,30,50\), but are not promoted to a proved theorem
until Lemma 2 is established rigorously.

\subsection*{Why finite-type entire-function methods do not prove this}

Step 307 blocks the direct finite-type Cauchy-Hadamard route: the zeta
functional equation gives
\[
  \log|\zeta(-R+i\eta)|=R\log R+O(R)
\]
off trivial-zero rays, so \(h=\zeta M(G_\star)\) is not finite type in the
order-one sense.  The lower-bound proof must therefore use the specific
Leibniz saddle and phase structure, rather than only global
entire-function type.
