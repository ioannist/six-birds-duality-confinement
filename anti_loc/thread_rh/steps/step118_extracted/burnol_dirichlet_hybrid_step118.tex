\section{Purpose}

The residual route after Steps 114--117 is
\[
\Xi^{BC}_{\ell,a}
=
\mathfrak B_{\ell,a}^*\Pi_{Y_a}\mathfrak B_{\ell,a}
\succeq 0.
\]
Step 117 defined a residual coefficient map
\[
R_N:Y_{R,N}\to\mathbb C^{I_N}
\]
and the visibility constant
\[
c_{R,N}
=
1-\|(I-P_N)M_{R,N}G_{R,N}^{-1/2}\|^2.
\]
The correction made in Step 112 is retained: \(c_N\) is not a column-norm estimate on a fixed map.
It is the spanning residual of a constructible, declared family.

Step 118 defines the hybrid family needed to make this residual source-readable.

\section{Two languages}

There are two useful languages.

\subsection{Burnol/co-Poisson language}

Burnol's carrier supplies the Sonine/co-Poisson geometry:
\[
L_a,\qquad K_a,\qquad P_a,\qquad Y_a,
\]
with
\[
P_a=Y_a^\perp
\]
for \(a<1\).  The Burnol-native atom family is generated by functions
\[
g_\nu\in C_c^\infty([a,A]),\qquad aA=1,
\]
satisfying the two Mellin vanishings
\[
\widehat g_\nu(0)=0,\qquad \widehat g_\nu(1)=0.
\]
The co-Poisson synthesis is schematically
\[
\mathcal C_a g(x)
=
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{g(x/n)}{n}-\widehat g(1).
\]
Let
\[
B_N:\mathbb C^{J_N}\to H_R
\]
be the synthesis map of a finite declared Burnol/co-Poisson atom family.

\subsection{Dirichlet/source language}

Heap--Soundararajan-style source strength is naturally expressed in short Dirichlet coefficient
space:
\[
\mathcal D_N a(s)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal N_N}a(n)n^{-s}.
\]
Let
\[
D_N:\mathbb C^{\mathcal N_N}\to H_R
\]
be the corresponding response synthesis map.

The character/source Gram has the form
\[
G_{\mathcal X,N}(m,n)
=
\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal X_N}
\lambda_{\omega,N}\omega(n)\overline{\omega(m)}.
\]
The source lower-frame gate is
\[
G_{\mathcal X,N}\succeq\gamma_NH_N.
\]

\section{The shadow problem}

Burnol atoms may be geometrically excellent but arithmetically unreadable unless they have
declared Dirichlet shadows.

\begin{definition}[Burnol-to-Dirichlet shadow map]
A Burnol-to-Dirichlet shadow map is a declared linear map
\[
A_N:\mathbb C^{J_N}\to\mathbb C^{\mathcal N_N}
\]
such that the response shadow
\[
D_NA_N
\]
approximates the Burnol atom synthesis \(B_N\).  Its normalized defect is
\[
\delta_{BD,N}
=
\|(B_N-D_NA_N)G_B^{-1/2}\|,
\qquad
G_B=B_N^*B_N.
\]
\end{definition}

The map \(A_N\) must be constructed from Mellin/co-Poisson data, support data, and declared
finite Dirichlet supports.  It may not be selected from the final zero-confinement inequality.

\section{Hybrid dictionary}

\begin{definition}[Source-readable hybrid dictionary]
The source-readable hybrid dictionary is
\[
D_N^{hyb}
=
\bigl[D_N,\;D_NA_N\bigr].
\]
Equivalently, it is the response range generated by ordinary short Dirichlet atoms together with
Dirichlet shadows of Burnol/co-Poisson atoms.
\end{definition}

This definition is deliberately stricter than simply adjoining \(B_N\) to the response dictionary:
a response atom that cannot be read by Hecke/Dirichlet characters improves visibility but does
not give a lawful source frame.

\section{Hybrid visibility theorem}

Let
\[
M_{R,N}:Y_{R,N}\to H_R
\]
synthesize the finite residual sector and set
\[
G_{R,N}=M_{R,N}^*M_{R,N}.
\]
Let \(P_B\) be the projection onto \(\operatorname{Ran}B_N\), and \(P_{hyb}\) the projection onto
\(\operatorname{Ran}D_N^{hyb}\).

Assume the Burnol atoms exhaust the residual sector with
\[
\epsilon_{B,N}
=
\|(I-P_B)M_{R,N}G_{R,N}^{-1/2}\|.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Hybrid visibility bound]
If the Burnol-to-Dirichlet shadow defect satisfies
\[
\delta_{BD,N}
=
\|(B_N-D_NA_N)G_B^{-1/2}\|,
\]
then the source-readable hybrid residual obeys
\[
\epsilon_{hyb,N}
:=
\|(I-P_{hyb})M_{R,N}G_{R,N}^{-1/2}\|
\le
\epsilon_{B,N}+\delta_{BD,N}
\]
after absorbing the declared normalization and finite-window comparison defects.
Consequently
\[
c_{hyb,N}
\ge
1-(\epsilon_{B,N}+\delta_{BD,N})^2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Approximate a normalized residual vector first by a Burnol atom combination, with error
\(\epsilon_{B,N}\).  Replace that Burnol approximation by its Dirichlet shadow, with normalized
error \(\delta_{BD,N}\).  The resulting vector lies in the hybrid source-readable range.  The
triangle inequality gives the residual bound.  The identity
\[
c_N=1-\|E_NG_{R,N}^{-1/2}\|^2
\]
then gives the lower bound on \(c_{hyb,N}\).
\end{proof}

\section{Effective source theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Hybrid source lower frame]
Assume
\[
G_{\mathcal X,N}\succeq \gamma_NH_N
\]
on the source-readable coefficient space and
\[
R_N^*H_NR_N\succeq c_{hyb,N}G_{R,N}.
\]
Then
\[
R_N^*G_{\mathcal X,N}R_N
\succeq
\gamma_Nc_{hyb,N}G_{R,N}.
\]
Thus the effective residual source strength is
\[
\Lambda_{R,N}=\gamma_Nc_{hyb,N}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The completed route requires
\[
\gamma_Nc_{hyb,N}\to\infty
\]
together with fixed/exhaustive residual-tail promotion.

\section{The new obstruction}

The new obstruction is not merely Burnol exhaustion and not merely Heap--Soundararajan source
strength.  It is the shadow-fidelity defect
\[
\delta_{BD,N}.
\]
If
\[
\epsilon_{B,N}\to0
\]
but
\[
\delta_{BD,N}\not\to0,
\]
then the Burnol atoms see the residual sector geometrically, but the Hecke/Dirichlet source
ladder cannot lawfully charge what it cannot read.

This is a membrane adequacy residual:
\[
\Xi^{BD}_N=(B_N-D_NA_N)^*(B_N-D_NA_N).
\]

\section{No-smuggling records}

The hybrid dictionary is accepted only if:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{H\arabic*.}]
\item the Burnol atom scale ladder is declared before target zero data;
\item Mellin vanishings and support records are fixed upfront;
\item the Dirichlet support \(\mathcal N_N\) is selected by arithmetic/refinement rules, not by the final inequality;
\item the shadow map \(A_N\) is computed from declared Mellin/co-Poisson data;
\item the character family \(\mathcal X_N\) is upstream-visible;
\item finite windows have fixed/exhaustive tail records;
\item the construction does not collapse into a de Branges/RKHS positivity condition.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

Step 118 gives the precise hybrid theorem:
\[
\epsilon_{hyb,N}
\le
\epsilon_{B,N}+\delta_{BD,N},
\qquad
\Lambda_{R,N}
=
\gamma_Nc_{hyb,N}.
\]
The route now has three independent obligations:
\[
\epsilon_{B,N}\to0,
\qquad
\delta_{BD,N}\to0,
\qquad
\gamma_Nc_{hyb,N}\to\infty.
\]

The next step is to construct the shadow map \(A_N\) explicitly from the co-Poisson/Mellin
formula and determine whether \(\delta_{BD,N}\) decays.
