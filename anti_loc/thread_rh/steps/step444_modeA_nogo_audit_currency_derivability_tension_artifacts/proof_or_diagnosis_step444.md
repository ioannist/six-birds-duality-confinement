# Step 444 Proof or Diagnosis

## Proof over the covered classes

### 1. Height-predictor audits

A height-predictor audit is RH-distinguishing only if it outputs or checks actual nontrivial zero heights. Step 441 showed that the concrete branch requires zeta evaluation or zero tables, while the formal branch has no heights and cannot distinguish target from control.

### 2. de Branges/kernel-positivity audits

A de Branges audit is RH-distinguishing only if the candidate structure function and kernel positivity are tied to the zeta target. Step 442 showed that concrete `E_xi` imports the target function or its positivity, while abstract `E` gives no kernel-positivity derivation.

### 3. Weil explicit-formula positivity audits

A Weil audit is RH-distinguishing through the explicit formula. Step 443 showed that concrete `W[f]` requires either zero heights or prime/von-Mangoldt data, while a formal `W` has no numerical or order-theoretic adequacy content.

These three classes cover the main audit-currency mechanisms named in the literature anchors: direct zero localization, de Branges Hilbert-space positivity, and Weil/Bombieri/Yoshida explicit-formula positivity.

## Literature support

Conrey 2003 and Bombieri 2000 describe the Hilbert-Polya graveyard pattern: candidate constructions either import arithmetic data or fail to produce the zeta-zero spectrum. Lagarias 2004 records the de Branges obstruction. Weil 1952 and Yoshida 1992 show that explicit-formula positivity is RH-equivalent but arithmetic-loaded through zero or prime sides.
