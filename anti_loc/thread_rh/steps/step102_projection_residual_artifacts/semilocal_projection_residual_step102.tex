\section{Purpose}

Step 101 identified the next analytic object:
\[
\mathcal R_S=U_SP_SU_S^{-1}-P_\infty .
\]
Here \(P_\infty\) is the archimedean Sonin/prolate projection and
\(P_S\) is the semilocal Sonin/prolate projection.  Step 102 derives
\(\mathcal R_S\) as a metric-projection residual.

The point is not to assert compactness. The point is to reduce compactness to
a precise off-diagonal block estimate.

\section{Semilocal setting}

Let \(H_\infty\) be the archimedean response Hilbert space and \(H_S\) the
semilocal response Hilbert space.  The semilocal Hardy--Titchmarsh transform
gives a response measure
\[
dm_S(\xi)=\left|\prod_{v\in S}L_v(1/2-i\xi)\right|^2\,d\xi.
\]
The semilocal Sonin theory supplies a Hilbertian isomorphism
\[
\theta_S:H_\infty\to H_S
\]
which maps the archimedean Sonin space onto the semilocal Sonin space.  It is
not automatically an isometry for every inner product used in the carrier.
Thus the transported semilocal orthogonality is encoded by the pulled-back
metric
\[
A_S:=\theta_S^*\theta_S
\]
on \(H_\infty\), or by the corresponding positive metric operator for the
chosen response model.

Let
\[
M:=\operatorname{Ran}P_\infty,\qquad N:=M^\perp.
\]
Write the block decomposition of \(A=A_S\) with respect to
\[
H_\infty=M\oplus N:
\qquad
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_{MM}&A_{MN}\\
A_{NM}&A_{NN}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\section{Metric projection residual}

\begin{theorem}[Metric projection residual formula]
Assume \(A=A^*>0\) is bounded and boundedly invertible on the completed core,
and \(A_{MM}\) is boundedly invertible on \(M\).  Let \(P_A\) be the
\(A\)-orthogonal projection onto \(M\), i.e. \(P_Ax\in M\) and
\[
\langle A(x-P_Ax),m\rangle=0,\qquad m\in M.
\]
Then, for \(x=m+n\in M\oplus N\),
\[
P_A(m+n)=m+A_{MM}^{-1}A_{MN}n.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_S=P_A-P_\infty
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&A_{MM}^{-1}A_{MN}\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The \(A\)-orthogonality condition says
\[
0=P A(x-y)
\]
where \(P=P_\infty\) and \(y=P_Ax\in M\).  If \(x=m+n\) and \(y\in M\), this is
\[
A_{MM}(m-y)+A_{MN}n=0.
\]
Solving gives \(y=m+A_{MM}^{-1}A_{MN}n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Compactness criterion]
Under the hypotheses above,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_S\text{ is compact}
\iff
A_{MN}=P_\infty A_S(I-P_\infty)\text{ is compact}
}
\]
and the same equivalence holds for Hilbert--Schmidt, trace-class, or Schatten
\(p\)-class membership whenever \(A_{MM}^{-1}\) is bounded.
\end{corollary}

\section{Consequences for \(\Delta_S\)}

Let the semilocal obstruction be
\[
\Delta_S=q_{\mathrm{Weil},S}-q_{\mathrm{pos},S}.
\]
The archimedean Connes--Consani residue is compact-after-quotient.  Bounded
semilocal transport preserves compactness of already compact pieces:
\[
K\text{ compact}\implies U_S K U_S^{-1}\text{ compact}.
\]
So the load-bearing new compactness question is not whether finite local
factors preserve compactness.  They do.  The question is whether the
orthogonality/projection change is compact:
\[
\boxed{
P_\infty A_S(I-P_\infty)\text{ compact?}
}
\]

If yes, then \(\Delta_S\) is compact up to finite-rank pole/null records and
declared tail terms.

If no, the noncompact block
\[
P_\infty A_S(I-P_\infty)
\]
is the exact anti-invariant sector that the Hecke/Dirichlet source ladder must
cover.

\section{No-free-multiplier warning}

A nonzero multiplication operator on a non-atomic \(L^2\)-space is not compact.
Therefore raw local-factor multiplication
\[
M_{|A_S|^2-1}
\]
cannot be treated as a compact correction merely because it is bounded.  It
must enter through paired/projection geometry, through a compact commutator,
through a quotient, or through source absorption.

\section{Status}

Step 102 earns the exact formula
\[
\mathcal R_S=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&A_{MM}^{-1}A_{MN}\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It does not prove compactness.  It converts the compactness problem into a
single off-diagonal metric-mixing estimate.
